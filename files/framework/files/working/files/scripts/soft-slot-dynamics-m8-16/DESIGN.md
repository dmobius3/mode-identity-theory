# M8.16 instrument design (draft v2.22, 2026-10-17)

A draft for review. It governs no run. Development proceeds under `BOUNDARY.md`, and every run is logged in `DEVLOG.md`. The development code is on the bench, in `instrument/` and `dev/`, and each run's entry hashes every file.

**Provenance.** The design is derived here from the frozen slot action, MIT's own pages and scripts, and the census. The M8.5-C2 protocol's public text was read in two passes:
- §§ 0 to 3, 5 to 9 and Addendum 1, during step 3, to state the overlap the ruling acknowledges;
- § 4.1 on 2026-10-15, after v1 of this design was written and hashed (DEVLOG.md, entry 1), to check a review unit's comparison.

Before v1 was written, two sources had already said that C2's production rule was exact to degree 4N: UB's step 3 review, which quoted § 4.1's "exact to degree 4N" and "ZERO aliasing", and C2's gate table, read in step 3, which names a "production 4N rule to rounding" whose arms drop to "2N points per angle". The Hopf product rule of §4, and its node counts, are derived here. It has the form of C2's production rule, a Hopf-coordinate product rule with 4N + 1 uniform points in each angle, with N + 1 Gauss–Legendre nodes in t where C2 used 2N + 1. No C2 code, no file under `m8_5c/design_inputs/` and nothing from the C2 room has been opened, and none will be.

## 1. What the instrument is

A stationary continuation and linear-spectrum record of named standing-wave branches of the frozen slot action, on its nontrivial sectors R1 to R8. It solves the stationary equation, continues branches, and reads the linearization's spectrum, with Krein signs and the Hamiltonian–Krein count. It does no time integration, and it runs no persistence test. That is xrodz's admission on #512, comment 18955679.

## 2. The law, derived from the slot action's §V

In the frozen units c = R = 1 and g₄ = 1, with −Δ on sections of E_ρ having levels k(k+2), k ≥ dist(ρ):
- **Standing waves** ψ = e^{iωT}φ solve F′(φ) = 0, where F(φ) = ½⟨(−Δ − λ)φ, φ⟩ + ¼∫|φ|⁴ and λ = ω².
- **The nonlinearity is pointwise:** |φ(x)|²φ(x), with |·| the fibre norm at each point of S³/2I. The global law, with ⟨φ, φ⟩ in place of |φ(x)|², is a different law (§V's own warning).
- **The linearization**, in the rotating frame ψ = e^{iωT}(φ + ζ), is ζ″ + 2iωζ′ + Lζ = 0, with Lζ = (−Δ − λ)ζ + |φ|²ζ + 2 Re(φ†ζ) φ. L is F″(φ), real-linear and symmetric for Re⟨·,·⟩.
- **The first-order form** is the Hamiltonian–Krein page's: B(x, x) = ‖ζ′‖² + ⟨Lζ, ζ⟩, with Ω(Ax, y) = B(x, y).

The code derives each of these from the §V functional, with its own symbolic check of the first and second variations. Nothing is copied from another instrument.

## 3. The discretization

- **Sections.** A section of E_ρ is ψ: S³ → W_ρ with ψ(γx) = ρ(γ)ψ(x) for γ ∈ 2I. That is MIT's convention: X = S³/2I with 2I acting on the left, and the right translations are the isometries.
- **The basis at level n.** It is f_{n,i,k}(x) = √(n+1) A_i π_n(x) e_k, where:
  - π_n is the spin-n/2 representation;
  - e_k is its weight basis;
  - A_i is an orthonormal basis, in the Frobenius form, of Hom_2I(V_n, W_ρ).

  The A_i are fixed canonically, so coefficients reproduce on any machine: the group average X ↦ (1/120) Σ_γ ρ(γ) X π_n(γ)⁻¹ is applied to the elementary matrices in a fixed order, and Gram–Schmidt keeps each image whose residual exceeds 10⁻⁸. The group average is then applied once more to each kept vector, and Gram–Schmidt rerun in the same order. Normalizing a small residual amplifies rounding, and this pass removes it: D1 measured an equivariance error of 7.4×10⁻¹¹ without it, and 6.7×10⁻¹⁴ with it. Their number must equal the multiplicity that 2I's characters give, and the nullspace of ρ(g)A = Aπ_n(g) at two generators of 2I stays as D1's independent count.
- **Orthonormality.** By Schur orthogonality the f_{n,i,k} are orthonormal in L² with the normalized measure, the factor √(n+1) normalizing them, and −Δ is diagonal, n(n+2) on level n.
- **The truncation** keeps complete levels n ≤ N.
- **Its symmetries (DERIVED, by Peter–Weyl).** Right translation acts on e_k by π_n(h), and the fibre group acts on the A_i, so the truncated space is invariant under both:
  - for the spinorial slots R1, R2, R6 and R8, which are of quaternionic type, the fibre group is Sp(1), generated by i and the quaternionic structure J. J maps level n to itself, through the real or quaternionic structure of V_n;
  - for R3, R4, R5 and R7, which are of real type, it is the phase U(1), with charge conjugation as a discrete symmetry besides.
- **The representations.** The slots are realized as in `soft_quartic.py`:
  - R1 = V₁, R3 = V₂, R6 = V₃, R7 = V₄ and R8 = V₅, restricted to 2I;
  - R2 = 2′, the Galois conjugate of V₁;
  - R4 = 3′ = Sym²(2′);
  - R5 = 4 = 2 ⊗ 2′.

  All are built from the 120 unit quaternions of 2I, and the Galois conjugate exchanges φ and −1/φ entrywise.

## 4. The quartic, exactly

- **The rule.** A product rule in Hopf coordinates, a = √t e^{iα} and b = √(1−t) e^{iβ}, under the uniform measure dt dα dβ/4π². It takes M ≥ 4N + 1 uniform nodes in each of α and β, and N + 1 Gauss–Legendre nodes in t ∈ [0, 1].
- **Why it is exact (DERIVED).** A monomial a^p ā^q b^r b̄^s of degree ≤ 4N:
  - integrates to zero over α unless p = q, and the uniform rule reproduces that, since |p − q| ≤ 4N < M; likewise in β;
  - what survives is t^p(1−t)^r with p + r ≤ 2N, and Gauss–Legendre with N + 1 nodes is exact to degree 2N + 1.
- **What the rule therefore gives.** Matrix coefficients of π_n are homogeneous of degree n in (a, b, ā, b̄), so for φ in levels ≤ N, F, its gradient (the Galerkin projection of the cubic) and its Hessian are integrals of degree ≤ 4N. The rule computes them exactly, to round-off. It is not collocation: nothing is evaluated at nodes and then interpolated.
- **Assembly.**
  - The energy and gradient are node sums, chunked. Each chunk's projection onto the basis, in the gradient, the Hessian-vector product and the fibre structure's matrix, is a GEMM, Pᴴ(·): einsum's accumulation over a large chunk lost about 10⁻¹³ on the rigid branch (UB; DEVLOG.md, entry 86).
  - The quartic's part of the Hessian, ∫ [|φ|² Re(δ₁†δ₂) + 2 Re(φ†δ₁) Re(φ†δ₂)], is a weighted Gram matrix, assembled by GEMM over node chunks. The quadratic part, −Δ − λ, is diagonal.
  - Newton can run matrix-free on Hessian-vector products, with the dense Hessian assembled only at recorded points.
- **An optional speed-up.** Pose 2I so that one of its 5-fold axes is the i-axis, which conjugation in SU(2) allows. Then, with M a multiple of 10, left multiplication by its cyclic subgroup of order 10 about that axis shifts α and β by π/5 and permutes the nodes. The integrands are 2I-invariant, so one node per orbit suffices. This is used only after a development test shows it equal to the full rule, to round-off.

## 5. The exact kernel, and why it is exact

The truncated space is invariant under right SU(2) and the fibre group, and the rule computes F exactly on it, so the discrete F is invariant to round-off. Differentiating that invariance twice at a discrete critical point puts every orbit tangent, T_a φ, iφ, and Jφ and iJφ in R2, in the Hessian's kernel to round-off (DERIVED). No zero is inserted, and no mode is projected away.

## 6. The solve and continuation

- **Newton**, on (φ, λ) at a fixed ‖φ‖², with the slices and gauges in a bordered system:
  - one row and column per vector of an orthonormal basis of the orbit tangents' span at the previous accepted point, its rank read by a pivoted QR and checked against the branch's isotropy (at C1, C2a and C2b the branch is a weight vector, so T_zφ is a multiple of iφ and the span drops a dimension);
  - the multipliers vanish at a solution;
  - Newton runs to its round-off floor, read from the iteration rather than fixed in advance: once the residual is below 10⁻⁸ times ‖(−Δ)φ‖ + λ‖φ‖ + ‖|φ|²φ‖, the size of the terms before they cancel, since λ's own rounding puts a branch's floor near ½ ulp(λ)‖φ‖, it steps until the residual stops halving, and keeps the smallest. An orbit tangent leaves L's kernel only by ‖L Tφ‖ = ‖T F′(φ)‖ ≤ ‖T‖ ‖F′(φ)‖ (D4's identity), and the record gives the floor, absolute and relative to that size, beside the kernel;
  - a point is accepted only if its floor is below 10⁻¹⁵ relative to that size. The halving rule alone also ends an iteration that converges linearly at ratio ½ or worse, and the floor breaks each symmetry chain, which then splits at about the square root of the breaking: at 10⁻¹³ that split can reach the cluster's gray zone at the ladders' upper ε, while at 10⁻¹⁵ it stays below a tenth of the margin (UB). The same bound applies to the unbordered residual, F′(φ) and the norm row without the multipliers' term, and to the multipliers themselves, so a small bordered residual with nonzero multipliers is never accepted. `newton.solve` enforces all three. The bound is provisional, open to revision on disclosed GO-list controls before registration (UA);
  - the spectrum is read on the unbordered L.
- **Continuation.** In ‖φ‖², with ε = λ − λ₀ an output and target ε reached by a secant, as in M8.15. A fold is handled explicitly: the branch ends at its located turning point, and never switches silently to another branch.
- **Seeds.** The theorems' germ directions at the smallest amplitude, scaled to the leading-order norm, and then the previous accepted point. At a higher N, the accepted point at the same ε and the next lower N, embedded exactly, since the canonical basis at a lower N is the leading block of the basis at a higher one. Its branch identity is checked, and an ambiguous identity is reported Unresolved (UA; `LADDER_PLAN.md`).
- **The guard.** The solver accepts only a named case on BOUNDARY.md's GO list. Before a freeze, the pyramid's and the census targets' seeds are constructed only by `sealed_seed_for_check`, for amendment 2's lowest-level check, and `seed_state` refuses them (BOUNDARY.md's note to amendments 2 and 3). The instrument enforces that scope (UB): `devtools.require('seedcheck')` marks the process, `sealed_seed_for_check` refuses outside a marked process, and inside one every solver path refuses: Newton, the gradient and the Hessians, `first_order`, `record` and `continue_to`. Every run appends its own DEVLOG.md entry: the script hash, the case and the outcome. Before the qualification, the registered path must replace the freeze file's mere existence with an explicit frozen allowlist, checked against the pre-registration's hash (UA); the registration specifies it (`registration/m8_16_task_details.md`, FIXED AT THE FREEZE). Today any case passes once a freeze file exists, and none exists.

## 7. The linear record

At each recorded point:
- the dense eigendecomposition of the Hamiltonian matrix A, of real size 4m;
- outside the zero cluster, groups of eigenvalues linked within τ(λ) = max(margin, κ_d √(u‖A‖ |λ|)), each typed real, imaginary or complex by its centre, against τ there. A defective eigenvalue splits on the scale √(u‖A‖ |λ|), which grows with |λ| while the margin does not (UB), and its group's centre moves only on the scale u‖A‖. Typing within τ moves no count of K: a quartet's pair within τ of an axis, read as lying on it, still contributes 2. An imaginary group carrying both Krein signs is therefore a collision within the resolution, possibly a quartet: K counts it, its type is reported unresolved with its largest |Re λ|, and a point is typed Elliptic only when every imaginary group is definite;
- the Krein signature of each imaginary group, from B on its eigenvectors where they span the group and B is nondegenerate on them. Both routes read nondegeneracy against |λ| ‖W‖, which bounds a unit eigenvector's Krein form since vᴴBv = λ̄ vᴴWv, and n(D)'s inertia is read against ‖B‖. Where the eigenvectors span the group but B is degenerate on them, the signature is reported unresolved: B is nondegenerate on the eigenspaces of imaginary eigenvalues, and the group's Schur subspace is the eigenvectors' span, so the Schur route would read the same form again. At a defective group, such as a Krein collision point, the eigenvectors do not span it and are B-isotropic, so the signature is read on the group's real invariant subspace, with its conjugate's, from an ordered real Schur form, and reported unresolved if that fails;
- the point's type: Hyperbolic when a group off the imaginary axis by more than τ at its centre lies outside the cluster's gray zone and the cluster matches the point's expected V₀, in dimension, Jordan ranks and symmetry leak; otherwise Elliptic only when nothing is unresolved, and Unresolved when anything is. A balanced K never stands in for the type (UA). Without an expected V₀ the type is not read;
- n(L) by inertia, at L's own cut, 1000 u‖L‖, with its own gray zone up to G_L = 10 times it (below). n(L) is unresolved where L's zero count differs from V₀'s chain count: ker A = ker L × {0}, so the two are equal (UB);
- the generalized kernel V₀, as the invariant subspace of the separated near-zero cluster, from an ordered real Schur form of A with that cluster in the leading block:
  - its dimension must equal the cluster's count;
  - it must contain the exact symmetry directions;
  - A on it must have the expected Jordan structure, read from the ranks of A and A² there;
  - the point's expected V₀, its dimension and those ranks, is stated before the point runs: for the controls, in `EXPECTED_V0.md`, derived from the theorems with M8.15's algebraic dimensions as the cross-check. A mismatch, or a symmetry leak above κ_L u‖A‖/gap, the gap being the smallest |λ| outside the cluster, is an unresolved item (UB). Where symmetry directions are given, a gap that is not finite and positive, or a bound above the cap, is an unresolved item, never a pass (UA). Without them, V₀ is checked on its dimension and ranks (UB), but only for a system declared free of symmetry directions: an empty list not so declared is an unresolved item, so a driver that loses its tangents cannot read Elliptic (UA);
  - the cluster's eigenvalues sit near (u‖A‖)^{1/j}, with u = 2.22×10⁻¹⁶, double precision's machine epsilon, as the code reads it (twice the unit round-off), and j the longest Jordan chain, since round-off splits a chain like a root of the breaking. So the kernel at round-off is read on L's inertia, and on the singular values of the leading Schur block and of its square. A chain's coupling grows with the amplitude, so the registered margin is the calibrated κ √(u‖A‖ max(m, m₀)) (below). DL's original claims keep the original margin, 30 (u‖A‖)^{1/2} with a gray zone a decade each side, and run unchanged under it (UB, UA);
  - if the cluster is not separated from the rest of the spectrum by a registered margin, V₀ and the index are reported unresolved, never forced. So are n(L) and n(D) when an eigenvalue of L, or of B on V₀, lies in its inertia gray zone.

  The Ω-annihilator of the nonzero eigenvalues' eigenvectors, which `identification_check.py` uses, is right only when those eigenvalues are semisimple. It fails at a defective one, such as a Krein collision point, where it returns a space larger than V₀ (UA's correction). DL shows the failure both ways. Built from exact eigenvectors, the annihilator has dimension 6 at a collision where V₀ has 4. Built from computed eigenvectors, the way `identification_check.py` builds it (there at 50 digits, here in double precision), it has V₀'s dimension but lies 2.2×10⁻⁹ from V₀, against 3.3×10⁻¹³ for the Schur V₀;
- n(D), and Theorem K, K = n(L) − n(D), checked at every point. A balance that fails is an unresolved item. So is a nullity of D that differs from the number of Jordan chains on V₀, its geometric multiplicity of zero: the two are equal, since Ω is nondegenerate on V₀ and B(x, y) = Ω(Ax, y), so a small partner energy is never taken for an exact zero (UA).

The test at a crossing, Theorem C's decomposition with the family's regularity, is built and checked on the Hamiltonian–Krein page's models and on the GO-list controls. It is not used on a target before the freeze.

## 10. Decisions, from the units' review of v1

1. **The continuation parameter.** ‖φ‖², with ε = λ − λ₀ as output and a secant to each target ε, as in M8.15, so the comparison with M8.15's ladders is like for like. Folds are handled explicitly (§6). Both units.
2. **Convergence.**
   - The development ladder is N ∈ {16, 24, 32, 40}.
   - A point's classification is converged when its spectral type, n(L), K, V₀ and its Krein structure (each imaginary group's negative-sign count) are unchanged between the two highest N and consistent along the ladder, and when its eigenvalues pass the separation test below (UA). The census's hard cases are near-zero genuine pairs.
   - Convergence in eigenvalues is judged by what could change the type. For each eigenvalue the record types (the slow band, and any mode of the next level near zero at a window's top), matched across the two highest N, its change, read as the remaining error estimated from the geometric rate where three cutoffs exist and as the last change otherwise, and never less than the larger margin of the two compared records, must be at most a tenth of its distance to the nearest type-changing event: the cluster's gray zone; the imaginary axis, for an eigenvalue off it; and the nearest imaginary eigenvalue of opposite Krein sign, for an imaginary one. Where numerical residuals or spectral sensitivity imply a greater uncertainty, that greater estimate is used; where it cannot be adequately controlled, the comparison is Unresolved (UA). Each compared eigenvalue carries that estimate beside it, unscored. Where the separation cannot be shown, or cannot be computed, the point is Unresolved (UA). The factor, a tenth, matches the record's gray-zone convention (§7). The rule and its factor were to be tested on the disclosed controls and on synthetic counterexamples, with mutation arms that must fail it, before the qualification's criteria freeze (UA). Both units confirm those tests, and the separation gate is closed (DEVLOG.md, entry 180; below). The relative change between the two highest N stays in the record as a quantitative diagnostic against 10⁻⁶, reported as failed where it fails: in D7's full ladders, at five upper-window points, C2b at ε = 128 and 132 and C3 at its top three, which stay failed under this rule (DEVLOG.md, entry 106; UA). The provisional fraction 10⁻⁶, calibrated at ε ≤ 2, is retired as a gate. Chosen after D7's full ladders, flagged, and registered before the qualification (UA, UB).
   - **The rule as built** (`instrument/convergence.py`; `SEPARATION_PLAN.md`; DEVLOG.md, entries 149 to 179). Both units confirm it, and the separation gate is closed (entry 180):
     - **Tested:** every typed group below half the band edge (the free frequency of the slot's lowest level above the lower cutoff), every Krein-negative or off-axis group wherever it lies, and each tested imaginary group's nearest group of opposite Krein sign (UB).
     - **Matched:** one to one and both ways (UA). Where a group's one-to-one match fails and it has candidates of its own class and Krein sign, of any size, it and those competitors are matched as a set, closed under the same test at every cutoff, its count taken with multiplicity: the set matches when that count agrees at every cutoff; its change is the bottleneck distance; its distance to an event is its nearest member's (UA, UB). Closures that share a group are one set, their union, so a set is the same whichever of its groups is read first, and every group to be read is read (DEVLOG.md, entry 219). A missing partner or a changed count reads Unresolved.
     - **The uncertainty:** the remaining error, geometric from three cutoffs and Unresolved where it does not contract above the margin; the margin as a floor; and ‖P_g‖ max(‖R_g‖, u‖A‖) from a projector that must be usable (UA).
     - **Its tests pass** (entry 169): the analytic projector; four converged synthetic ladders; eight counterexamples, each reading at every cutoff a type its limit lacks, all Unresolved; ten mutation arms red. On the controls, D7's sample converges at every point, and the ladders at N = 16 and 24 converge with no wrong type (entries 159 to 172).
     - **The order of the groups** (entries 218 to 223). Both units confirm the change, and the separation gate, closed in entry 180, is closed again (entry 234).
       - The dry run found that each closure read alone, in stored order, with the groups already read passed over, turned a control's verdict on the eigensolver's output order (entry 218).
       - Linking two groups when either lies within twice the other's nearest candidate removed that, but joined sets through the higher cutoff's unmatched groups to the whole spectrum. It was not adopted (entry 219).
       - Under the merged closure, every saved control reading keeps its verdict under 24 shuffles of every cutoff's groups. The sets as built, kept as the arm `one_way_set`, turn C2a's ε = 2 in each of the three readings that carry it (entries 220 to 222).
       - Two development verdicts change, both to converged: the dry run's C2a at ε = 2, and C2a's ladder at ε = 32, where two closures share a group (entries 220 and 228).
     - **Its stated limits (UA, UB), for the registration:** a slow rate hidden under a faster one is beyond any reading of a few cutoffs (F at 15 margins, and R from two cutoffs, read converged); the rule rests on the leading rate dominating at the highest cutoffs, and the controls' rates there are at most 0.033. The factor is an operational adequacy test, not a bound on every spectral error (UA). The upper-window runs did not identify each next-level mode near zero, and every group below half the band edge is tested, so none is omitted.
   - The gates freeze before any sealed control runs. Both units.
