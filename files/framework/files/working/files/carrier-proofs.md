# The Projective Carrier: Supporting Proofs

**Type:** Result
**State:** Closed
**Status (2026-10-01):** Derived. The proofs of Propositions 4.1, 4.2, 6 and 7 of The Projective Carrier, each under a copy of its statement, with the remarks that followed three of them. Among bands of Proposition 3's class with a connected smooth locus only the conic bands have a rigid tilt as a Friedrichs mode (4.1). Under the Friedrichs realization the exact $`2/R^2`$, kept as the simple first positive level, does not force geodesic laps, since near every conic band suitably chosen moves of one lap off its line within the plane, away from the pinch, keep it (4.2). With the edge fixed, the bowtie at the cone point is locally area-minimizing: a competitor that re-maps the band only within a small ball about it has strictly more area unless it covers the carrier's image there exactly once (6). Coupled to changes elsewhere, no continuous finite-piecewise-smooth carrier-class competitor with the edge fixed as parametrized has less area than the conic band, and one ties only by covering it once (7).

## Proposition 4.1

The statement, as it stands in [The Projective Carrier, §VI](projective-carrier.md#vi-lemma-4-the-2r2-mode-is-the-bands-tilt):

**Proposition 4.1 (DERIVED; Lemma 4's converse).** Let $`M`$ be a band in Proposition 3's class, a closed region of the round $`\mathbb{RP}^2(R)`$ with a piecewise-smooth edge whose only singular points are corners and two-sector pinches, with a connected smooth locus. Let $`e`$ be a unit vector of the three-space that holds the band's great sphere. If the tilt $`x_e = \langle x, e\rangle`$ is an eigensection of the Friedrichs realization on $`M`$, then $`M`$ is a conic band $`M(W)`$, for some $`0 < W < \pi R/2`$, with its pinch at $`[e]`$, and the eigenvalue is $`2/R^2`$. Conversely, on $`M(W)`$ the tilt $`x_p`$, with $`p`$ a lift of the pinch, is the $`2/R^2`$ mode (Lemma 4).

*Proof.*
1. On the smooth locus $`x_e`$ solves $`-\Delta x_e = (2/R^2)\,x_e`$, and it lies in the form domain (Theorem 2, step 2). So it is an eigensection exactly when its conormal derivative vanishes on every smooth arc of the edge. At an edge point $`x`$ with unit tangent $`T`$ and unit conormal $`n`$, that derivative is $`\langle e, n\rangle`$. It vanishes exactly when $`e`$ lies in the plane of $`x`$ and $`T`$: when the great circle tangent to the edge at $`x`$ passes through $`\pm Re`$.
2. Away from $`\pm Re`$ the great circles through $`\pm Re`$ are the integral curves of the nonvanishing field $`e - \langle e, x\rangle x/R^2`$, and the condition says the edge's tangent is parallel to that field. So every smooth arc lies on a projective line through $`[e]`$; an arc through $`[e]`$ stays on one line, since distinct lines through $`[e]`$ have distinct tangents there.
3. Two distinct lines through $`[e]`$ meet only at $`[e]`$. At a corner or pinch away from $`[e]`$, every arc that meets there would lie on the one line through that point and $`[e]`$, so the arcs would continue one another, since arcs that overlapped would bound no part of $`M`$, and the point would not be singular. So every singular point of the edge is $`[e]`$, every arc runs along its line to $`[e]`$, and the edge is a union of whole projective lines through $`[e]`$. A single line is excluded by Proposition 3, which rules out a smooth closed geodesic edge.
4. Those lines cut $`\mathbb{RP}^2`$ into double sectors at $`[e]`$, and $`M`$ is a union of some of them. Two adjacent sectors cannot both lie in $`M`$, since the line between them would then not be edge, and two that are not adjacent share only $`[e]`$. With the smooth locus connected, $`M`$ is one double sector, of opening $`2W/R`$ with $`0 < 2W/R < \pi`$: the conic band $`M(W)`$ with its pinch at $`[e]`$, whose tilt $`x_e`$ is the $`2/R^2`$ mode by Lemma 4. ∎

## Proposition 4.2

The statement, as it stands in [The Projective Carrier, §VI](projective-carrier.md#vi-lemma-4-the-2r2-mode-is-the-bands-tilt):

**Proposition 4.2 (DERIVED; the seed does not force the laps).** Fix $`0 < W < \pi R/2`$, so that $`2/R^2`$ is $`M(W)`$'s simple first positive level (§V). Take the lune $`L`$ between two meridians through $`N`$, a lift of $`p_c`$, as $`M(W)`$'s fundamental domain on its great sphere, with $`s`$ the distance from $`N`$ and $`\phi`$ the longitude, $`\lvert\phi\rvert \le W/R`$. For a smooth profile $`V`$ supported in a closed interval $`J \subset (0, \pi R)`$, let $`M_V`$ be the band with fundamental domain

```math
D_V = \Big\{-\frac{W}{R} \le \phi \le \frac{W}{R} + \frac{V(s)}{R\sin(s/R)}\Big\},
```

one lap moved off its line within $`\mathbb{RP}^2`$ by $`V`$ along the normal, to first order, and the band unchanged near $`p_c`$. For small $`V`$, $`M_V`$ is in Proposition 3.1's class. Let $`\lambda(V)`$ be the first positive level of its Friedrichs realization. Then:
1. $`\lambda(0) = 2/R^2`$; near $`0`$, $`\lambda`$ is analytic along every finite-dimensional family of profiles, and

```math
\lambda'(0)[V] = \frac{3}{4WR^3}\int_0^{\pi R}\Big(\sin^2\frac{s}{R} - 2\cos^2\frac{s}{R}\Big)V(s)\,ds.
```

2. Take $`b`$ supported in $`J`$ with $`\lambda'(0)[b] \neq 0`$; one exists, since the density vanishes at only two points. For every profile $`a`$ supported in $`J`$ there is an analytic $`\tau(t)`$, $`\tau(0) = 0`$, with $`\lambda\big(ta + \tau(t)\,b\big) = 2/R^2`$ for small $`t`$.
3. If $`a`$ is not a multiple of $`b`$, then for small $`t \neq 0`$ the band $`M_{ta + \tau(t)b}`$ keeps the exact $`2/R^2`$ as a simple first positive level, its moved lap is not a geodesic arc, and its $`2/R^2`$ mode is not a rigid tilt.

So under the Friedrichs realization the exact $`2/R^2`$, kept as the first positive level, forces a singular point that carries the twist (Corollary 2.1) but not geodesic laps.

*Proof.*
1. *The realization.* Sections of $`\mathcal L`$ over $`M_V`$ are the functions on its preimage $`D_V \cup (-D_V)`$ in the great sphere that are odd under $`-I`$, so they are fixed by their restrictions to $`D_V`$. The points $`\pm N`$ have zero capacity, so the Friedrichs form domain is $`H^1(D_V)`$ and the realization is the Neumann Laplacian of $`D_V`$, as for $`M(W)`$. Its bottom is $`0`$, carried by the constants. On $`M(W)`$ the level $`2/R^2`$ is simple, with the next level above it (§V).
2. *Analyticity.* The map $`\Phi_V(s, \phi) = \big(s,\ -W/R + (\phi + W/R)\,w_V(s)/w_0\big)`$, with $`w_V(s) = 2W/R + V(s)/(R\sin(s/R))`$ and $`w_0 = 2W/R`$, carries $`L`$ onto $`D_V`$, keeps $`s`$, and is the identity where $`V = 0`$, in particular near $`\pm N`$. Pulled back by it, the Dirichlet and $`L^2`$ forms of $`D_V`$ become forms on $`H^1(L)`$ and $`L^2(L)`$ whose coefficients are rational in the parameters of a finite-dimensional family of profiles, with denominators bounded away from zero, and uniformly equivalent to the lune's own. Multiplying by the square root of the Jacobian $`w_V/w_0`$, which is analytic in the parameters and bounded away from zero, makes the $`L^2`$ form the fixed product of $`L^2(L)`$ and keeps $`H^1(L)`$ as the form domain. A simple isolated level of such a family is holomorphic in each complexified parameter, with derivative the derivative of the two forms on the normalized eigenfunction (T. Kato, *Perturbation Theory for Linear Operators*, [Springer, Classics in Mathematics, 1995](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-642-66282-9), Chapter VII), and so jointly holomorphic, by Hartogs' theorem.
3. *The first variation.* The mode $`u_0 = x_p/R = \cos(s/R)`$ does not depend on $`\phi`$, so it pulls back to itself, and its forms on $`D_V`$ are integrals in $`s`$ against $`R\sin(s/R)\,w_V(s)\,ds`$, whose derivative in $`V`$ is $`V(s)\,ds`$. With $`\int_{M(W)} u_0^2\,dA = 4WR/3`$,

```math
\lambda'(0)[V] = \frac{3}{4WR}\int_0^{\pi R}\Big((\partial_s u_0)^2 - \frac{2}{R^2}\,u_0^2\Big)V\,ds,
```

which is 1's formula.

4. *The level set.* 2 follows from 1 by the implicit function theorem. For 3, $`ta + \tau(t)b \neq 0`$ when $`t \neq 0`$, since $`a`$ is not a multiple of $`b`$. The moved lap then agrees with its meridian for $`s`$ below $`J`$ and leaves it inside $`J`$. A geodesic that agrees with a meridian on an arc is that meridian, so the lap is not a geodesic arc. $`M_V`$ is then not a conic band, so by Proposition 4.1 no tilt is its mode. Simplicity and the first-positive position persist for small $`t`$ by continuity. ∎

- The formula in 1 agrees with Hadamard's formula. M. M. Fall and T. Weth prove that formula for the first nonzero Neumann eigenvalue of a domain with $`C^2`$ boundary in a Riemannian manifold, as a one-sided derivative that is two-sided when the eigenvalue is simple (*Critical domains for the first nonzero Neumann eigenvalue in Riemannian manifolds*, [J. Geom. Anal. 29, 3221–3247 (2019)](https://doi.org/10.1007/s12220-018-00115-w); Proposition 3.1 of arXiv:1803.07592). The lune has corners at $`\pm N`$; the pullback in step 2 does not move them, so the proof does not need that theorem.

## Proposition 6

The statement, as it stands in [The Projective Carrier, §IX](projective-carrier.md#ix-fixed-edge-stability):

**Proposition 6 (DERIVED).** Let $`D`$ be the strip $`\lvert y - \pi R/2\rvert \le \delta`$ of the band's rectangle about the collapsed fibre, and $`F`$ the unbent carrier's map, which carries $`D`$ onto the bowtie $`B`$ at $`p_c`$, two opposite sectors of the carrier's $`\mathbb{RP}^2`$ of radius $`\delta`$. Take $`\delta < \varepsilon < \pi R/2`$, and let $`f`$ be a Lipschitz map of $`D`$ into the open ball of radius $`\varepsilon`$ about $`p_c`$ with $`f = F`$ on $`\partial D`$. Then the area of $`f`$, counted with multiplicity, is at least that of $`B`$, with equality exactly when $`f`$ covers $`B`$ once: $`f_\#[D] = F_\#[D]`$, almost every point of $`B`$ has one preimage, and the image off $`B`$ has zero area. So a genuine two-dimensional resolution of the pinch, or a bending of a sector, changes the image current and has strictly more area, while a map that only reparametrizes the strip, or sends some of it onto curves, which carry no area, does not.

*Proof.* The ball lifts isometrically to the ball of radius $`\varepsilon`$ about $`N`$ in $`S^3(R)`$, so work there, with currents in $`S^3`$ and $`\Sigma_0`$ the great sphere of the double lune.
1. *Orientation through the fibre.* With $`X(y, w) = R(\cos(y/R)\cos(w/R), \cos(y/R)\sin(w/R), \sin(y/R))`$ in $`\Sigma_0`$, lift $`D`$ near $`N`$ by $`F(y, w) = X(y, w)`$ for $`y \le \pi R/2`$ and $`F(y, w) = X(y, -w)`$ for $`y \ge \pi R/2`$, which runs over the pole into the tip of $`-L`$. Then $`\det[F, \partial_y F, \partial_w F] = -R\lvert\cos(y/R)\rvert`$ on both halves, so $`F_\#[D] = [B]`$, with one orientation of $`\Sigma_0`$ on both sectors: the one carried through the fibre, which is how a competitor supported near $`p_c`$ carries it. Carried the long way round, through the smooth locus and the seam, the sectors come out oppositely oriented, since $`-I`$ reverses $`\Sigma_0`$'s orientation.
2. *A calibration.* Let $`C`$ be the great circle of $`\Sigma_0`$ at distance $`\pi R/2`$ from $`\pm N`$. The great spheres through $`C`$ foliate the ball by totally geodesic leaves, $`\Sigma_0`$ among them. Their unit normal field $`\nu`$ has zero divergence, the leaves being minimal, so $`\omega = \iota_\nu\,\mathrm{vol}`$ is closed in the ball. It has comass one and restricts to each leaf as that leaf's area form; orient $`\nu`$ so that $`\omega`$ is positive on $`[B]`$.
3. *The inequality.* $`T = f_\#[D]`$ and $`[B]`$ have the same boundary, $`F_\#[\partial D]`$, so $`T - [B]`$ is a cycle with compact support in the ball, which is convex, and bounds there, and $`\omega`$, being closed, takes the same value on both. By the area formula the area of $`f`$ is $`\int N(f, x)\,dx \ge \mathbf M(T)`$, where $`N(f, x)`$ counts preimages. Hence $`\mathrm{Area}(f) \ge \mathbf M(T) \ge \omega(T) = \omega([B]) = \mathrm{Area}(B)`$.
4. *Equality.* If $`\mathrm{Area}(f) = \mathrm{Area}(B)`$, both inequalities are equalities. The first makes the preimage count $`N(f, \cdot)`$ equal to the absolute value of $`T`$'s multiplicity almost everywhere. The second makes $`T`$'s tangent planes the leaves' own almost everywhere. For a function $`g \ge 0`$ of the leaf parameter that vanishes near $`\Sigma_0`$, tangency gives $`\partial(T \llcorner g) = (\partial T) \llcorner g = 0`$, since $`\partial T`$ lies in $`\Sigma_0`$. So $`T \llcorner g`$ is a normal cycle with compact support in the ball, the boundary of a normal current there, and its mass, $`\omega(T \llcorner g)`$, is zero. Hence $`T`$ is supported in $`\Sigma_0`$, where it is $`[\Sigma_0] \llcorner m`$ for an integer function $`m`$, and $`T - [B]`$ is a cycle with compact support in the open cap $`\Sigma_0 \cap`$ ball, which the constancy theorem makes zero. So $`T = [B]`$ and $`f`$ covers $`B`$ once. Conversely, a map that covers $`B`$ once has area $`\mathrm{Area}(B)`$. ∎

- The twist does not enter. The map $`P`$ that turns each leaf about $`C`$ onto the hemisphere of $`\Sigma_0`$ containing $`N`$ is 1-Lipschitz, fixes $`\partial T`$ and carries the ball into the open cap, where the constancy theorem makes $`P_\# T = F_\#[D]`$. So $`\mathrm{Area}(f) \ge \mathbf M(T) \ge \mathbf M(P_\# T) = \mathrm{Area}(B)`$ however the fibre orients the two sectors. The equality clause needs no calibration either. $`P`$ is an isometry along the leaves and kills their normal, so equality makes $`T`$ tangent to them. For almost every $`t > 0`$ the piece $`T \llcorner \{\theta \ge t\}`$, with $`\theta`$ the leaf angle, then has zero slice (coarea formula) and is a cycle. $`P`$ carries it to a cycle with compact support in the open cap, which is zero, and since $`P`$ loses no mass on $`T`$, that piece has mass zero; likewise for $`\theta \le -t`$. So the proposition holds for an untwisted pinch with the same image, as the second variation above does; the calibration of step 2 is the route that happens to use the fibre's orientation.

## Proposition 7

The statement, as it stands in [The Projective Carrier, §IX](projective-carrier.md#ix-fixed-edge-stability):

**Proposition 7 (DERIVED; least area with the edge fixed).** Let $`M'`$ be a competitor for $`M(W)`$: a continuous, finite-piecewise-smooth map of a Möbius band into $`\mathbb{RP}^3(R)`$ whose boundary map is $`M(W)`$'s edge as parametrized and whose core maps to the generator of $`\pi_1(\mathbb{RP}^3)`$. Then, with area counted with multiplicity,

```math
\mathrm{Area}(M') \ge 4WR = \mathrm{Area}(M(W)).
```

Equality holds exactly when $`M'`$ covers $`M(W)`$ once: almost every point of $`M(W)`$ has one preimage, and the image off $`M(W)`$ has zero area. Embeddedness is neither assumed nor needed, and the change from $`M(W)`$ may sit anywhere on the band, near $`p_c`$ and elsewhere together.

*Proof.*
1. *A closed surface.* In the chart of Proposition 6's proof, the edge's two passes through $`p_c`$ join the rays at longitudes $`W/R`$ and $`\pi - W/R`$, and those at $`-W/R`$ and $`\pi + W/R`$; between the passes the edge traverses each projective line whole, smoothly through the seam. The lune between longitudes $`W/R`$ and $`\pi - W/R`$ is bounded by one meridian from $`N`$ to $`S`$ and the other back; its vertex $`N`$ joins the first pair of rays, and its vertex $`S`$, carried to $`N`$ by $`-I`$, the second. So the complementary lune, mapped as a disc, spans the same edge as parametrized, with area $`2\pi R^2 - 4WR`$ (the fence on least area; Proposition 5). Gluing $`M'`$ to it along the edge gives a continuous, finite-piecewise-smooth map $`f`$ of a Möbius band plus a disc, a projective plane $`\Sigma`$, into $`\mathbb{RP}^3`$, and, area being additive on the domain pieces, $`\mathrm{Area}(f) = \mathrm{Area}(M') + 2\pi R^2 - 4WR`$.
2. *Its class.* $`\Sigma`$'s fundamental group is generated by the core of $`M'`$, which $`f`$ sends to the generator, so $`f`$ is an isomorphism on $`\pi_1`$. With $`H^*(\mathbb{RP}^n; \mathbb Z_2) = \mathbb Z_2[a]/(a^{n+1})`$, $`f^*`$ carries the generator $`a`$ of $`H^1(\mathbb{RP}^3; \mathbb Z_2)`$ to the generator $`b`$ of $`H^1(\Sigma; \mathbb Z_2)`$, and $`\langle a^2, f_*[\Sigma]\rangle = \langle b^2, [\Sigma]\rangle = 1`$. So $`f_*[\Sigma]`$ is the nonzero class of $`H_2(\mathbb{RP}^3; \mathbb Z_2)`$, a projective plane's, whose intersection number with a projective line is 1 mod 2. The images under $`f`$ of the pieces' edges and corners, and of the points where a piece has rank below 2, have zero area, the last by the area formula, and the projective lines meeting a set of zero area form a set of measure zero. By parametric transversality, almost every projective line $`\ell`$ is therefore transverse to $`f`$ on every piece and misses those images. For such $`\ell`$ the number $`n_f(\ell)`$ of points of $`\Sigma`$ that $`f`$ sends into $`\ell`$ is finite and congruent to that intersection number mod 2, so odd and at least 1.
3. *Crofton.* In $`\mathbb{RP}^3(R)`$, Crofton's formula expresses the area of a surface as a fixed multiple of the invariant measure of the projective lines that meet it, each weighted by its number of meetings (L. A. Santaló, *Integral Geometry and Geometric Probability*, [2nd ed., Cambridge University Press, 2004](https://doi.org/10.1017/CBO9780511617331)). For a finite-piecewise-smooth map it holds with multiplicity, $`\mathrm{Area}(f) = c\int n_f(\ell)\,d\ell`$, by applying it to the regular immersed sheets of each piece and summing through the area formula. A projective plane has area $`2\pi R^2`$ and meets almost every line exactly once, which fixes $`c`$. So $`\mathrm{Area}(f) \ge 2\pi R^2`$, and $`\mathrm{Area}(M') \ge 2\pi R^2 - (2\pi R^2 - 4WR) = 4WR`$.
4. *A tie.* By steps 2 and 3, $`\mathrm{Area}(M') = 4WR`$ exactly when $`n_f(\ell) = 1`$ for almost every line. Say a line meets $`f`$ twice if $`f`$ sends two or more points of $`\Sigma`$ into it, and call a set of lines thick if it contains a nonempty open set, so that it has positive measure. If every line of a thick set meets $`f`$ twice, then $`n_f \ge 3`$ almost everywhere on it, $`n_f`$ being odd, and $`\mathrm{Area}(f) > 2\pi R^2`$. So in a tie no thick set of lines meets $`f`$ twice.
5. *Flat pieces.* Let $`x`$ be a point interior to a piece where $`f`$ has rank 2, so that $`f`$ embeds a small disc about $`x`$, and suppose the disc's second fundamental form at $`f(x)`$ has $`A(v, v) \neq 0`$ for a unit tangent $`v`$. Project $`S^3(R)`$ from the origin of $`\mathbb R^4`$ onto its affine tangent hyperplane at a lift of $`f(x)`$. In this affine chart projective lines are straight, and the metric is Euclidean at $`f(x)`$ with vanishing first derivatives, so $`A`$ is also the chart's second fundamental form there, and the disc is a graph over its tangent plane whose height along $`v`$ is $`\tfrac12 A(v, v)s^2 + O(s^3)`$. The line parallel to $`v`$ through the point at a small height $`h`$ on the side toward which the disc curves crosses it twice, near $`s = \pm\sqrt{2h/\lvert A(v, v)\rvert}`$, transversally, since the slope there is about $`A(v, v)s \neq 0`$, and so does every line close enough to it: a thick set that meets $`f`$ twice. So in a tie $`A = 0`$ at every such point, and each connected open set of them maps into a single projective plane: on a local lift to $`S^3(R)`$, $`A = 0`$ makes the unit normal a constant vector $`\nu`$ of $`\mathbb R^4`$, and the lift, orthogonal to $`\nu`$, lies in the great sphere $`\nu^\perp`$.
6. *One plane, covered once.* Let $`U_1`$ and $`U_2`$ be open sets of such points, on one piece or two, the lune included, that map into different projective planes $`P_1`$ and $`P_2`$. Each image is open in its plane, so there are points $`a \in f(U_1)`$ off $`P_2`$ and $`b \in f(U_2)`$ off $`P_1`$. The line through $`a`$ and $`b`$ lies in neither plane, so it crosses $`P_1`$ only at $`a`$ and $`P_2`$ only at $`b`$, transversally, and every line close to it meets $`f(U_1)`$ near $`a`$ and $`f(U_2)`$ near $`b`$, at two different points: a thick set that meets $`f`$ twice. So in a tie every such point maps into one plane, the carrier's $`\mathbb{RP}^2`$, since the lune's points do. The lines not in that plane, almost all of them, meet it once each, and the invariant measure carried to the meeting point is invariant under the isometries that keep the plane, so it is a positive multiple of the plane's area. By step 2 almost every line misses the images of the pieces' edges and corners and of the points of rank below 2, so it meets $`f(\Sigma)`$ only where it meets the plane, and when $`n_f = 1`$ that point has exactly one preimage under $`f`$. So the lines through the points of the plane with other than one preimage form a null set, and those points have zero area. The lune maps once onto its own region and nowhere else, so almost every point of $`M(W)`$ has one preimage under $`M'`$, and the image of $`M'`$ off $`M(W)`$ has zero area: $`M'`$ covers $`M(W)`$ once. Conversely, a competitor that covers $`M(W)`$ once has area $`4WR`$, by the area formula. ∎

- **Pu's inequality.** P. M. Pu proves that every Riemannian metric on the projective plane has area at least $`(2/\pi)`$ times its systole squared (*Some inequalities in certain nonorientable Riemannian manifolds*, [Pacific J. Math. 2, 55–71 (1952)](https://doi.org/10.2140/pjm.1952.2.55)). Every non-contractible loop of $`\Sigma`$ maps to one of length at least $`\pi R`$, so Pu's inequality would give the same number, $`2\pi R^2`$, for the glued surface once extended to the metric $`f^*g`$, which is only piecewise smooth across the glued edge even for a smooth competitor; that extension is not done here. The bound rests on Crofton's formula.
