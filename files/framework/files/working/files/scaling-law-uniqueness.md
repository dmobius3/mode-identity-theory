## The Precedent: Gleason's Theorem

Gleason (1957) proved that on a Hilbert space of dimension ≥ 3, the only probability measure consistent with the lattice of projections is the Born rule: p = |⟨ψ|φ⟩|². The theorem doesn't derive Born from dynamics. It shows Born is the unique measure compatible with the structure.

The MIT analog: show that C(Θ) × (√Ω)⁻ⁿ is the unique sampling rule compatible with the bounded, closed topology of S¹ = ∂M ↪ S³, ∂S³ = ∅.

The precedent is exact for one piece only. C(Θ) is a normalized mode intensity (∫₀¹ C dΘ = 1), so a Gleason-type measure-uniqueness statement is the right tool there. The hierarchy (√Ω)⁻ⁿ is dimensional analysis (Buckingham π), not a measure; the factored form is a separation-of-variables result (the Schur separation, proved below). The three rows are three different rigidity claims of three different types, and only the phase operator is Gleason-shaped; the analogy is motivation for that one piece, not scaffolding for the whole. This note keeps them separate rather than letting one banner blur the kinds of argument or lend the open separation the standing of a measure theorem.

## The Argument Shape

**Step 1:** Establish the space of candidate measurement rules.

Any rule maps (phase position Θ, manifold depth n, hierarchy Ω) to a dimensionless observable ratio. The output space is ℝ⁺. The input space is {Θ ∈ k/120} × {n ∈ ℤ or ℤ/2} × {Ω ∈ ℝ⁺, finite}.

**Step 2:** Constraint 5 narrows the output but does not factor it. A dimensionless output from dimensionless inputs need not be a product: $`f(\Theta) + g(\Omega, n)`$ and $`f(\Theta)g(\Omega, n) + h(\Theta, \Omega)`$ are equally dimensionless. The factored form

```math
\frac{A}{A_P} = f(\Theta) \cdot g(\Omega, n)
```

is supplied only by constraint 6 (Schur decoupling), and only if Θ ⊥ s extends to Θ ⊥ (Ω, n). The homothety reduction (below) delivers the independence of the coordinates, but independence is not separability: the no-cross-term step needs boundary-mode uniformity, so at this step the factorization is a well-supported target, not a closed result.

**Step 3:** Apply constraint 1. f(Θ) satisfies the anti-periodic BC, whose eigenfunctions are trigonometric: the BC kills every polynomial, exponential, or rational candidate and admits the tower 2sin²((2m+1)πΘ). The constant zero mode is the extension-dependent background (the spatial-eigenvalue ground, distinct from the temporal harmonic ground of the cosmic wave) and carries no phase structure. The BC fixes the family but not the member; the member is selected physically. For background observables, isotropy and orthogonality exclude the higher harmonics, the same conditions that fix the cosmic background wave, leaving the lowest nonconstant mode, the first-positive eigenmode at 2/R², whose intensity is 2sin²(πΘ). So f(Θ) = C(Θ) = 2sin²(πΘ) is the first-positive-mode intensity, not the constant zero mode. This is one operator read at different positions: cosmology samples a single well, the mass sector samples Kostant exponent sets on the same mode. Three inputs enter here that the BC alone does not give: the harmonic selection (isotropy + orthogonality, for background observables), the normalization (∫₀¹ C dΘ = 1 in the flat Θ measure fixes the leading 2), and the intensity readout that squares the mode. The first two are selection principles and should be named, not absorbed into "the BC forces it"; the third is a conditional theorem on adopted measurement premises ([Linear readout](#linear-readout-two-routes-and-a-no-go)).

**Step 4:** Apply constraints 2 and 4. g(Ω, n) encodes dilution from Planck to observation on a bounded domain. Ω is the hierarchy ratio, finite by constraint 2; the self-dual point sits at √Ω by constraint 4 (its identification with the observer is the physical reading); n counts depth. Step 5 fixes the function.

**Step 5, resolved for integer depth.** Read n as the length-dimension of the observable. Λ is 1/length² (n = 2), H₀ and a₀ are 1/length (n = 1).

*What units give, and what they do not.* Units fix the admissible dimensional form: an observable normalized to its Planck value is $`(\ell_P/R)^n`$ times a dimensionless function of the one available ratio,

```math
\frac{A}{A_P} = (\sqrt{\Omega})^{-n} \cdot F(\sqrt{\Omega}).
```

Units do not fix $`F`$. Buckingham-π supplies the dimensional prefactor and leaves the dimensionless function free, which is the standard situation once a dimensionless ratio is available at all. Concretely $`(\sqrt{\Omega})^{-n}\,(1 + \epsilon \ln \sqrt{\Omega})`$ is dimensionally identical and survives constraints 2, 4 and 5. So the pure power is not a units result, and the earlier reading of this row as "form and base both follow from dimensions" quietly used the stronger premise that the observable's natural scale is exactly $`R^{-n}`$. That premise is homogeneity, not dimensional analysis.

*What fixes the power.* Exact homothety covariance, which Step B below already establishes on the spectral side: under $`R \mapsto \lambda R`$ the Hodge Laplacian scales as $`R^{-2}`$ with the unit-radius eigenspaces R-independent. An admissible observable of definite weight n satisfies $`A(\lambda R) = \lambda^{-n} A(R)`$, so with $`x = R/\ell_P`$ its normalized function obeys

```math
f(\lambda x) = \lambda^{-n} f(x) \quad \text{for all } \lambda, x > 0.
```

Setting $`x = 1`$ gives $`f(\lambda) = f(1)\,\lambda^{-n}`$ directly, hence $`f(x) = C x^{-n}`$ and $`A/A_P \propto (\sqrt{\Omega})^{-n}`$. No regularity assumption enters: the one-point substitution does the whole job, so none of the pathological solutions of the unconstrained Cauchy equation arise. The log correction dies here rather than at the units step, because rescaling generates an extra $`\epsilon \ln \lambda`$ that exact covariance forbids.

*Definite weight n is an admissibility condition, not a theorem.* The rigidity holds within the homogeneous weight-n sector, not over every conceivable observable. A non-homogeneous functional of the spectral weights, say $`\hat a(1)/R^2 + \hat a(2)^2/R^4`$, carries no single n and sits outside the statement. Dimensional analysis does not forbid such sums; the weight condition excludes them, and saying so keeps the scope of the claim visible rather than letting units appear to do more than they can.

Constraint 2 keeps Ω finite so the ratio is well-defined, and constraint 4 places the self-dual point at R/ℓ_P (read as the observer's scale by physical postulate), the same number the dimensions deliver. This closes the integer-depth case on Step B's homothety, without needing boundary-mode uniformity.

**Scope: one scale or two.** The "R is the only scale" reading holds for surface and space modes, which dilute from the geometric curvature radius R via Ω_Λ = (R/ℓ_P)². Edge modes do not: they reference the kinematic horizon, Ω_H = (c/Hℓ_P)², so their base is c/H, not the geometric R. The two coincide only through Ω_H ≈ Ω_Λ at the present epoch, which the framework records as an observed coincidence, not a derived identity (see [calibration-structure.md](calibration-structure.md)). So the *form* (√Ω)⁻ⁿ holds in every sector, with units fixing the prefactor and definite weight the power, but the *identification* √Ω = R/ℓ_P is a surface/space statement; for edge modes √Ω = c/(Hℓ_P) is a separate anchor. Deriving Ω_H = Ω_Λ would collapse the two and turn Λ (n = 2) and H₀ (n = 1) into a second route to R, alongside the mass sector.

**Step 5, the coupling exponents.** A dimensionless coupling has length-dimension 0, so dimensions fix no Ω dependence. The exponent the couplings do carry, 1/60 for EM and 1/120 for the strong and weak forces, comes from the grid ladder selection rule: the carrier character sets the phase grid, the confinement target sets the exponent grid, and the exponent is one cell of that grid, 1/\|grid\|. That rule is the framework's, established as a rule but not forced: the representation-theoretic route derives the two grids from the central sign, but the exponent 1/\|grid\| on Ω is a homothety weight of 2/\|grid\| that no spectral scaling supplies ([The Ladder Rule](#ladder-rule-grids)). Separately, which grid a given coupling occupies is the motivated ladder assignment. So the exponents are fixed once those two framework rules are accepted, rather than fitted to the couplings they reproduce, which is what "supplied, not open" is claiming and all it is claiming. What this paper's standard would add is lifting the ladder rule from established to forced, which needs a weight principle the spectral side does not supply.

## Proof: the Schur Separation

*Argues constraint 6: within the observable class, the form separates. Two gaps are kept explicit. The class is a premise, not shown to exhaust the physical observables. And the no-cross-term step needs boundary-mode uniformity, which Step C proves conditionally, on adopted premises; what is established without them is the weaker statement that Θ and the spectral data are independent coordinates.*

The factored form was the one structural row left open. Dimensionlessness does not separate Θ from (Ω, n), and Lemma 8 separates Θ only from the spectral parameter s. The gap is the step from Θ ⊥ s to Θ ⊥ (Ω, n). It closes once (Ω, n) is recognized as spectral-block data: the metric scale R entering the eigenvalues, nothing more.

**Proposition (within the observable class; conditional on boundary-mode uniformity).** Let A be an admissible single-position observable: a quantity built SU(2)-equivariantly from the Hodge-Laplace spectral data of S³/2I and the first-positive anti-periodic mode on the Möbius boundary S¹ (narrow band below the critical width, W < πR/2), under the adopted Friedrichs extension (1e; a bridging extension would need δ₀/R > 2/e). Then, *provided every contributing spectral block carries that mode's profile C(Θ) times a fixed spectral weight* (Step C, boundary-mode uniformity, a conditional theorem), A/A_P = C(Θ) · g(Ω, n) with no cross-term, where C(Θ) = 2sin²(πΘ) is the boundary-phase factor and g(Ω, n) the scale-and-depth factor.

**Step A, positional reduction.** By Peter-Weyl, sections of a natural bundle split into SU(2)-isotypic blocks V_l; by Schur an equivariant operator is scalar on the irreducible factor, with an operator allowed on the multiplicity space. For the functions of the Hodge-Laplace spectrum used here, that multiplicity action is scalar on each eigenspace, which gives each block its scalar weight a(l) (the spectral-zeta reduction, the-mirror Step 2); Step C's lemma does not rely on it. On the bulk S³/2I the right-SU(2) action is transitive, so no continuous bulk position survives (the S³/2I half of Lemma 8: the twisted heat kernel is constant on each fiber diagonal). The only continuous positional coordinate left in A is the Möbius boundary phase Θ.

**Step B, scale reduction.** The round metric of radius R is R² times the unit metric, so the Hodge Laplacian scales as R⁻² and a_R(l) = â(l)/R², with the unit-radius eigenspaces and their internal bases R-independent. The sampler is taken at unit radius, R∂_ν, so it adds no power of R ([The Projective Carrier](projective-carrier.md), §XI, 1d). The whole (Ω, n) dependence is therefore carried by the scalar weights a_R(l): Ω = (R/ℓ_P)² is their common scale, and n is the homothety weight, the power of R the observable carries (its length-dimension). On the spectral zeta this reads as Z(s) ↦ λ²ˢ Z(s) under R ↦ λR: a pure s-power, staying inside the spectral block, never touching the eigenspace basis where Θ lives. This is the precise sense in which the depth index rides the spectral block.

**Step C, separation (a conditional theorem).** Extract the homothety weight: A/A_P = (√Ω)⁻ⁿ · K, where K depends on Θ (positional) and on R-invariant spectral data (eigenvalue ratios, multiplicities, the bundle label n). Lemma 8 gives Θ ⊥ s for the zeta parameter, and for this equivariant family the eigenvalue-multiplicity data are fixed by the 2I character table and the Peter-Weyl decomposition (no isospectral freedom is invoked, and no claim that the spectrum fixes the geometry), so they are Θ-blind. That makes Θ and the spectral data **independent coordinates**.

Independence is necessary but not sufficient. A joint function K = C(Θ)·F(n) + h(Θ, n) is consistent with independent coordinates: nothing so far excludes the cross-term h. A factored observable algebra does not exclude it either. Write K over the spectral blocks of Step A, with P_l the projections onto them. Equivariance makes K block-diagonal, K = Σ_l K_l(Θ) with K_l = P_l K P_l, and where the spectral operators act as scalars on each block (Step A) every block is a profile times its projection:

```math
K = \sum_l f_l(\Theta) \otimes P_l .
```

Any algebra that holds the Θ-functions and the P_l holds every such sum, block-dependent profiles f_l included, and since the P_l commute with the SU(2) action, which does not move Θ, the sums lie in the commutant too. The target this step carried until 2026-09-23, A_obs = A_Θ ⊗ A_spec with no operator of the form h(Θ, λ_l) in the commutant, therefore could not close the separation: the tensor-product algebra is where those operators live.

What excludes the cross-term is a statement about the blocks, not the algebra. **Equivalence (DERIVED).** K factorizes as C(Θ) times a spectral operator A if and only if every block carries C(Θ) times a fixed operator:

```math
K = C(\Theta) \otimes A \iff K_l(\Theta) = C(\Theta)\, A_l \ \text{ for every } l, \qquad A_l = P_l A P_l .
```

Sufficiency: pull C(Θ) out of the sum. Necessity: K fixes each K_l block by block, and the factored form gives K_l = C(Θ) A_l. The one assumption is block-diagonality, which equivariance supplies. Nothing requires an irrep to occur once: where one repeats, A_l acts on its multiplicity space and the lemma holds as stated. Where the blocks are scalar, A_l = a_l P_l and the condition reads f_l(Θ) = a_l C(Θ). A block with K_l = 0 contributes nothing and meets the condition with A_l = 0.

## Linear readout: two routes and a no-go

The phase operator's weight is the registered intensity, the squared amplitude. Its form came by analogy with Malus's law, I = I₀cos²θ, the intensity a polarizer passes. Here it follows from named properties of the readout, in a fixed setting.

- **Domain.** At each position the readout is a function Q on the whole fiber V that holds the sampled state, not only on the line the state traces (full-fiber domain, adopted).
- **Rule.** Q is the same at every position of the observable domain, on both [sampling grids](../../../README.md#the-sampling-grids) (position independence, adopted); the edge pair spans both, a₀ reading on the 120 grid and H₀ on 60R. The state-free rule of [R2](sampling-kernel-symmetry.md) does not supply this: that rule may depend on the band's geometry, which varies with Θ.
- **State.** The sampled state in block l is granted separated, ψ₁(Θ)v_l; the grant assumes the first-positive projection of the sampler's [transfer operator](sampler-first-test.md), from which Step C's reduction derives it when the first-positive eigenspace is simple. With ψ₁ normalized so that |ψ₁|² = C, a readout Q = c‖x‖² gives K_l(Θ) = C(Θ)·c‖v_l‖², a linear readout at a fixed weight.

**Theorem A (invariance and minimal degree).** Let a group G act orthogonally on V, irreducibly over ℝ. Let Q ≥ 0 be a G-invariant polynomial with Q(0) = 0, of the lowest degree among nonconstant nonnegative G-invariant polynomials that vanish at 0 (minimal degree, adopted). Then Q(x) = c‖x‖², with c ≥ 0.

*Proof.* ‖x‖² is a nonconstant nonnegative invariant vanishing at 0, of degree 2, so Q has degree at most 2. Q has no linear part: if its linear part ℓ had ℓ(x) ≠ 0, then Q(tx) = tℓ(x) + O(t²) would be negative for small t of the right sign. So Q(x) = ⟨x, Bx⟩ with B symmetric, and invariance under the orthogonal G makes B commute with G. B lies in the commutant, a real division algebra by Schur's lemma, so ℝ[B] is a finite-dimensional commutative domain, hence a field, and B's minimal polynomial is irreducible over ℝ. B is symmetric, so that polynomial splits over ℝ. Irreducible and split, it is linear: B = c·1, and Q ≥ 0 gives c ≥ 0. ∎

Minimal degree here means a quadratic readout: ‖x‖² always qualifies and no nonzero readout is linear, so the lowest degree is always 2. It is stated over nonnegative invariants because a readout is nonnegative. The earlier wording compared all invariants, which was unsatisfiable wherever a fixed vector exists and agreed with this one everywhere else; positivity now removes the linear part, so the theorem needs no fixed-vector hypothesis and no evenness.

**The group each block carries.** Every sampled state is a section of the orientation line L tensored with the pullback of a flat bundle E_τ over S³/2I ([postulate bridge](postulate-bridge.md#a-sampler-reading)), so its fiber is V_τ ⊗ L. L's sign, −1, lies in every block's G and fixes no nonzero vector; this is evenness, Q(−x) = Q(x), DERIVED from the anti-periodic BC. The bundle is E_τ = (S³ × V_τ)/2I with [p, v] = [gp, τ(g)v], so a readout that is one function Q of the fiber coordinate through every lift is well defined only if Q(τ(g)v) = Q(v), and τ(2I) lies in G too. That is the quotient reading, adopted: the postulate bridge reads the 120 deck translates as presentations of the same quotient data, and the invariance fails if the sampler's preferred lift carries physical information. Where a real-type sector's field is complex, phase blindness ([R1](sampling-kernel-symmetry.md)) adds U(1). With that G every fiber is irreducible, and B is scalar in every cell. The sectors are typed by Frobenius–Schur indicator, −1 for the spinorial irreps and +1 for the rest.

**Theorem B (orthogonal sample additivity).** Let Q ≥ 0 be even, with Q(x + y) = Q(x) + Q(y) whenever x and y are orthogonal (orthogonal sample additivity, no ground), on V of real dimension at least 3. Then Q(x) = c‖x‖², with c ≥ 0.

*Proof.* By Rätz's theorem ([Aequationes Math. 28, 35–49, 1985](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF02189390)), an orthogonally additive Q on a real inner-product space is α(x) + a(‖x‖²), with α and a additive. Evenness gives α = 0. Positivity makes a nonnegative on [0, ∞), so a is increasing there, since a(s + t) = a(s) + a(t) ≥ a(s), and an additive function monotone on an interval is linear: a(t) = ct. No continuity is assumed. ∎

Orthogonality here is real, under which x ⊥ ix, a stronger hypothesis than complex orthogonality. Every fiber in the table has real dimension at least 3 except two. The complex line is covered with phase blindness: Q(z) = g(|z|), real orthogonality of a and ib gives g(√(a² + b²)) = g(|a|) + g(|b|), so h(s) = g(√s) is additive and nonnegative on [0, ∞), h(s) = cs, and Q = c|z|². The orientation line has no orthogonal pairs, so route B says nothing there; the Λ_top row's fiber needs route A. On the ruled carrier under plain restriction the untwisted integer-spin blocks carry neither L's sign nor τ(−I) = −id, so evenness is not derived there and route B does not reach them. Under the adopted transverse sampler route B reaches every block but the real trivial one.

**Proposition C (the no-go).** Q(x) = c′‖x‖⁴ is positive, even, phase-blind, invariant under every orthogonal group, the same for every state and position, and normalizable through c′. It fails both routes: its degree is 4, and ‖x + y‖⁴ = (‖x‖² + ‖y‖²)² exceeds ‖x‖⁴ + ‖y‖⁴ for nonzero orthogonal x and y. On a separated state it reads K_l ∝ C(Θ)². So the framework's other stated properties do not give a linear readout, and minimal degree and orthogonal additivity are two sufficient additions, not the only ones.

**What it comes to.** Both routes force Q = c‖x‖² at each position. Neither forces c to be the same at every position; that is position independence, adopted. So linear measurement reduces to position independence plus either minimal degree, which here means a quadratic readout, or orthogonal sample additivity. The theorems are proved. The premise sits at the grade of its weakest input, which is adopted through route A (the full-fiber domain, the quotient reading, a quadratic readout, position independence, and R1 where a real-type field is complex; positivity natural) and ungrounded through route B (orthogonal additivity, with evenness where the fiber supplies it). The shared boundary mode, uniformity's other condition, reduces to the first-positive projection (Step C), an adopted premise that carries the keystone: the separated state grants it rather than derives it. Together they make uniformity a conditional theorem (Step C).

### The Ladder Rule: the Grids, Not the Weight

The representation-theoretic row, worked: the central sign fixes the grids, and nothing in the spectral scaling fixes the exponent.

- **The grids (DERIVED under the image reading).** For a nontrivial irrep $`\rho`$ of $`2I`$, Schur's lemma makes $`-1`$ act as $`\pm 1`$. If $`+1`$, $`\ker\rho`$ contains $`\lbrace\pm 1\rbrace`$, and since $`2I/\lbrace\pm 1\rbrace \cong A_5`$ is simple and $`\rho`$ is nontrivial, $`\ker\rho = \lbrace\pm 1\rbrace`$. If $`-1`$, $`\ker\rho`$ meets $`\lbrace\pm 1\rbrace`$ trivially and maps isomorphically onto a normal subgroup of $`A_5`$; a copy of $`A_5`$ would make $`2I = \ker\rho \times \lbrace\pm 1\rbrace`$ and give $`2I`$ a second element of order 2, so $`\ker\rho`$ is trivial. So $`\rho`$ acts through a group of order $`[2I : \ker\rho]`$: 60 for the four nontrivial integer-spin irreps and 120 for the four spinorial ones, the sheet counts of $`\mathbb{RP}^3`$ and $`S^3`$ over $`S^3/2I`$. Under the scope note's image reading, then, a nontrivial irrep in the integer-spin class has image order 60 and one in the spinorial class 120, and these are the only two grids. Assigning each carrier or target role to one of the two classes, by its action rather than the mediator's own spin, stays the motivated ladder assignment. No image has order 240, so the 240 alternative for $`\alpha_s`$ is excluded under this reading and survives only under another.
- **The exponent is a weight.** A coupling proportional to $`\Omega_\Lambda^{-1/D}`$ has homothety weight $`2/D`$ at fixed $`\ell_P`$, $`1/30`$ on the 60 grid and $`1/60`$ on the 120, though its length-dimension is 0. Step B turns a definite weight into a power law and does not fix the weight; the ladder rule's content is that weight, equivalently the exponent $`1/D`$ on $`\Omega_\Lambda`$.
- **The Casimir route does not reach it.** Every natural Laplace or Casimir operator on the round quotient, twisted by a flat bundle or not, scales as $`R^{-2}`$: the representation changes which levels occur and their multiplicities, the central-sign split included, not the degree. The primed spectral zeta $`\zeta_\Delta(s) = \sum_{\lambda_k > 0} \lambda_k^{-s}`$, continued meromorphically where necessary, scales as $`R^{2s}`$ for every $`s`$, so the rule returns only by choosing $`s = -1/D`$, which puts it in by hand. The quotient trace formula ([below](#trace-formula-route)) carries $`1/\lvert G\rvert`$ as the normalization of the group average, in the identity term and in the class fractions $`\lvert C\rvert/\lvert G\rvert`$ alike: a linear counting factor, which no identity found turns into the power $`R^{-2/\lvert G\rvert}`$. The scalar shells are in [The Molien Shells](molien-shells.md); the first nonconstant one, at $`168/R^2`$, carries 13 modes, a count with no known link to the well at 13.
- **Candidates.** A weight principle could come from a volume share of $`\ln\Omega_\Lambda`$ on the faithful cover, from a geometric mean over the image group (the same premise in algebraic form, and a multiplicative average where the readout is additive), or from one McKay step, $`(\sqrt{\Omega_\Lambda})^{1/30}`$, which gives EM's exponent but needs half a step for the 120 rows. Each imports a premise at least as strong as the rule.

So the route is PARTIAL: it derives the grids and leaves the weight to an independent principle, and the ladder rule stays ESTABLISHED, not forced.

## Note: The Trace-Formula Route

The spectral approach and the representation-theoretic approach may not be independent. On S³/2I the identity that ties them is the finite-group quotient trace formula, $`\mathrm{Tr}_{S^3/2I}\,F(\Delta) = \lvert G\rvert^{-1}\sum_{g \in G}\mathrm{Tr}(g\,F(\Delta_{S^3}))`$ with $`G = 2I`$, grouped by its 9 conjugacy classes: Peter-Weyl gives the eigenvalues, and the class terms carry the group data, the translation lengths of the quotient's closed geodesics among them. With the integer hierarchy settled by units plus exact homothety and the coupling exponents supplied by the ladder rule, the identity's possible role narrowed to the representation-theoretic route, if the finite geodesic-length set fixed the 1/\|grid\| scaling. It has not been found to: the 1/\|G\| is the normalization of the group average, in the identity term and the class fractions alike, a linear factor that no identity found turns into an exponent ([The Ladder Rule](#ladder-rule-grids)).

**Source:** The Selberg connection was flagged during review of Schepis, *Observer-Resonance Theory* (2026), Ch. F §3.3-3.4.
