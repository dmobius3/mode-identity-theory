# ⏳ The Slot Dynamics in Time

**Type:** Program
**State:** Active
**Status (2026-11-21):** Opened, with its method stated in a dated note before the prototype ran: a time arm for M8.16.1's Galerkin instrument that keeps the continuous symmetry, the controls with their pass lines and stopping rules, and the rule that chooses the stepper. The prototype's code was committed before its registered run, with the readings of the design it fixes in a second note, and the run is recorded in a third: under the registered readings nothing stopped, controls 1 to 9 passed for both steppers, the FFT cubic is adopted, and the rule chose the splitting for production, with Verlet kept as the reference. Verlet's pass of control 9 rests on reading 7's window: read with ω_max itself, as § 8 names it, Verlet fails control 9 in R2 at N = 12, where the controls would have stopped. The qualification comes after OpenWave's ruling on time integration.
**Summary:** The slot fields' standing-wave branches evolved in time under the frozen slot action, on a discretization that keeps its continuous symmetry: first the instrument and its controls, then whether the small-amplitude spectral types predict the response to perturbation.
**Inputs:** `slot-action.md`, `slot-ground-states.md` (Theorem D), `slot-dynamics-census.md` (its rows, the rigid families among them), the Standing-Wave Branches (§§ 2.2 to 2.4, 3.3, 4, 5 and 6.2), M8.16.1's instrument (`scripts/soft-slot-dynamics-m8-16-1/instrument/`, frozen at `d1c0ec8`), the finite-element integrator (`scripts/soft-slot-dynamics-m8-15x/m8_15x_solver/`), and the #512 [boundary of 2026-10-15](https://github.com/openwave-labs/openwave/discussions/512#discussioncomment-18955679) and [answer of 2026-11-01](https://github.com/openwave-labs/openwave/discussions/512#discussioncomment-19339563).

PROGRAM, with its method stated before the prototype ran. The [Standing-Wave Branches](../../dynamics/files/standing-wave-branches.md) leave the branches' dynamics to a sequel, which "needs a new discretization first, since the Galerkin method was qualified without time integration" (§ 6.2). This page carries that work. Its first step builds the discretization: M8.16.1's Galerkin instrument, unedited, with a time arm that keeps the phase, right-SU(2) and, in the quaternionic slots, Sp(1) charges exactly in exact arithmetic, and the instrument's exact cubic computed by FFT, adopted only if it agrees with the instrument's own cubic to round-off. The method, the controls and their pass lines are in [`scripts/slot-dynamics-time/DESIGN.md`](scripts/slot-dynamics-time/DESIGN.md). It is MIT's own computation, outside OpenWave's column.

**Related:** [The Slot Action](slot-action.md), [The Slot Ground States](slot-ground-states.md), [The Slot Dynamics Census](slot-dynamics-census.md), [The Hamiltonian–Krein Identification](hamiltonian-krein-identification.md), [Standing-Wave Branches](../../dynamics/files/standing-wave-branches.md).

## Notes

**Note (2026-11-20), on the instrument.** Before the prototype runs, the method is stated here. The sequel to the Standing-Wave Branches evolves the frozen slot action in time on M8.16.1's Galerkin instrument, at its frozen commit, unedited, with Störmer–Verlet as the reference and a splitting with the exact linear flow weighed beside it by a rule fixed in advance. The time arm is new code, so neither M8.16.1's qualification nor the admission of 2026-10-15 carries to it. The scheme keeps the phase, right-SU(2) and, in the quaternionic slots, Sp(1) charges exactly in exact arithmetic, and its energy is measured and gated. The cubic is computed by the instrument's rule, through two-dimensional FFTs on its uniform angular nodes, adopted only if it agrees with the instrument's own cubic to round-off. The controls, with their pass lines and stopping rules, are in the [design note](scripts/slot-dynamics-time/DESIGN.md) committed beside this note, unedited once the prototype starts. Before this note only a timing of the existing cubic, and a check of one identity of its basis at random points, have run. From the start the record names which results rest on the new code: the stepper and the FFT cubic. This prototype tests the instrument, not the persistence or instability of a census branch. The qualification that follows reads the small-amplitude frequencies at every soft-slot branch, with its criteria frozen in its own note after OpenWave's ruling on time integration. It is MIT's own computation, outside OpenWave's column: it reads none of the census's twelve unclassified points and says nothing about the closed spectral chassis.

**Note (2026-11-20), on the prototype's code.** The prototype's code is committed beside the design note before its registered run, in [`scripts/slot-dynamics-time/`](scripts/slot-dynamics-time/): the FFT cubic, the two steppers, the controls of § 8 with their dry run, and control 11's cross-check, all new code. Every seed, size, duration and normalization that the design leaves to committed code is fixed there before anything registered runs, with sixteen readings of the design, and every output carries them. One of them reads § 8's stopping rule, whose sentence names the failures that stop the controls without naming the stepper: a failure of control 4, 7, 8 or 9 by the splitting alone disqualifies the splitting under the rule of § 4, and the controls go on with Verlet, the reference, while a failure by Verlet, a failure of control 1 or 2, or a run past C̄ stops them. The design note stands as written. Development ran only on development seeds and at N ≤ 12, each run logged with what it showed in [`DEVLOG.md`](scripts/slot-dynamics-time/DEVLOG.md); none of it is a control, and none of it is in the record.

**Note (2026-11-21), the prototype's record.** The registered run went once, on 2026-11-21, into [`record/`](scripts/slot-dynamics-time/record/), in 2 h 57 min. It ran from the code's commit, `d14d06be`, which was also the run's HEAD, with every file the interlock names committed and unchanged, and it imported M8.16.1's instrument through its pin at `d1c0ec8` and control 11's finite-element modules through their own pin. Every result rests on new code, the stepper and the FFT cubic with the controls that read them, which neither M8.16.1's qualification nor the admission of 2026-10-15 covers. Under the sixteen readings nothing stopped: controls 1 to 9 passed for both steppers, the FFT cubic is adopted, and the rule of § 4 chose the splitting for production, with Verlet kept as the reference. Verlet's pass of control 9 rests on reading 7 (control 9's window, below).

- **The floors.** The dry run, the record's first entry, measured every floor with the cubic switched off before any pass line was read: below 6 × 10⁻¹⁵ for the cubic's symmetries and, on the multi-level states, for reversibility, the charges, the equivariance and the energy, and below 3 × 10⁻¹² on the rigid waves.
- **Controls 1 and 2, the FFT cubic,** which both steppers share. Over sixty states in R1 to R4 at N = 8 to 32 it agrees with the instrument's cubic within 4 × 10⁻¹⁵, normalized, against a line of 10⁻¹¹, and within 2 × 10⁻¹⁵ in a basis whose intertwiners carry a complex phase per copy. Each of the six mutation arms turns it red, the weakest, the aliased grid, at 2.5 × 10⁻². Its energy equals the instrument's within 7 × 10⁻¹⁴, and it is that energy's gradient within 9 × 10⁻¹⁶.

- **Cost, control 10,** in R4. At N = 32, under Verlet, the FFT path takes 0.069 s per step and 0.110 s with every diagnostic, at a peak resident memory of 0.08 GB; the dense cubic takes 7.43 s and 13.0 s, at 0.88 GB, 107 times slower per step. The complete step is under § 7's 0.17 s: a T = 1000 run at § 5's small-amplitude step, about 21,000 steps, would take about 40 minutes, and one at control 10's own step, 0.0162, takes 1.9 hours, against 224 hours on the dense path.
- **Control 11, report-only.** From the same initial data in R4 at ‖c‖² = 10⁻⁶, the finite-element Verlet at mesh level 2 and the Galerkin instrument at N = 12 part to a relative distance of 0.033 at t = 19.6, and at most 0.036 along the run. The mesh's seven lowest-level eigenvalues lie a relative 5.3 × 10⁻⁴ above the exact 48 on average, a frequency error that accumulates to a phase of about 0.036 over the run, so the distance reads as the mesh's error rather than the time arm's (INFERRED). The finite elements keep the phase and Sp(1) but break right SU(2), so they are not an authority on the Galerkin instrument (§ 8).
- **Control 9's window.** Reading 7 applies the band where h·ω̄ ≤ 0.5, with ω̄ § 5's bound, whose free part is N(N+2). In R2 at N = 12 the truncation holds the single level n = 7, so at h₀/2, where h·ω̄ = 0.80, h·ω_max is at least 0.41, from that level's n(n+2) = 63. At every step the record observes, every second step of that run, it is at most 0.49: the relative energy error there is at most 1.55 × 10⁻³, and H ≥ ½·63‖c‖² on that level, so ‖c‖² ≤ 2.02 from H(0) = 63.30, and § 5's two inequalities, with m/r = 8/2, give the bound (DERIVED). A replay of that run on its registered seed, which reproduces the record's energy errors to round-off and observes every step, puts it at most 0.43 throughout ([`DEVLOG.md`](scripts/slot-dynamics-time/DEVLOG.md), entry 14). Verlet's energy ratio there is 4.73, outside the band, and the next two are 3.91 and 3.95.
