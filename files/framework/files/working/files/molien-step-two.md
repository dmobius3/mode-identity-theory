## 0. What the test assumes, and the prescription it freezes

The contract freezes a spectral-transfer prescription, P1, as the thing tested. P1 is not yet a derived consequence of MIT's ontology. It rests on two stated assumptions, and step two tests their consequences, not the assumptions themselves.

1. **S³/2I is the source domain.** Its 2I-invariant spectrum fixes which source modes exist, the "global boundary condition" of [CMB Anomalies](../../../../cosmos/files/cmb-anomalies.md) §VI, and the sky forms under the flat-FLRW propagation of §V, where 2I does not act. So 2I selects the spectrum because it acts on the source domain, and identifies no points of the sky because it does not act there.
2. **The translation to the sky keeps spectrum, not pattern.** It keeps each shell's wavenumber and variance and discards its spatial pattern. The first assumption does not imply this one: if the sky field were the source field itself, the quotient's identifications would sit in its correlation function whichever way it is written (a sum over eigenmodes and a sum over images give the same function), and matched circles would follow.

The CMB page's §II names S³/2I as the spatial manifold, and [The Molien Shells](molien-shells.md) §III asks what an observer at a point of S³/2I sees. If MIT holds that light crosses S³/2I itself, the model is the countermodel below, not P1.

**The prescription, P1 (spectral selection).** Shell N enters the sky as an isotropic flat-space field at wavenumber k_N = √(N(N+2))/R, carrying the variance the source domain gives it (§2); everything else is flat ΛCDM. Nothing sits below the first shell, but the projection is not sharp: each shell feeds a range of multipoles through the transfer function, so low multipoles are suppressed rather than emptied (§8 records two crude pre-freeze estimates of how far). No points are identified, so the circle observable is N/A under P1 (§5). Only the shell counts reach the sky; step one's patterns stay source structure. This test carries no parity or alignment observable. It neither closes nor supports the covariance route the CMB page keeps open for them (§III), which would need its own stated source-to-sky map and its own contract.

**The countermodel, rejected: P2, a genuine spatial quotient.** If light crossed S³/2I itself, the sky would repeat under 2I. Flat space cannot carry that: a finite group of Euclidean isometries fixes the centroid of every orbit, so 2I cannot act freely there, and no complete flat manifold has fundamental group 2I. The standard realization is the curved Poincaré space of radius R, which brings back the curved projection D_M = R sin(χ/R) that §V withdrew as a coordinate error. Its circles appear once the distance to last scattering exceeds the inscribed radius R_i = πR/10, half the shortest closed geodesic πR/5: for R below 10χ*/π, which is above 40 Gpc whenever χ* exceeds 12.6 Gpc. MIT's routes give R_i from 1.9 to 6.3 Gpc, under half of χ* near 14 Gpc (the value behind §IV's multipole figures), far from the marginal regime of the published dispute over the Poincaré space (Caillerie et al. 2007). Planck 2013 XXVI puts the dodecahedron's R_i above 1.0 χ_r in a positively curved universe (a likelihood bound, marginalized over orientation), and Key et al. 2007, whose Section IX names the Poincaré dodecahedral space the "soccer ball universe", rule that model out with a circle search. The countermodel is recorded to show what the other reading would cost. Its failure is not the reason P1 is frozen.

## 1. The question

Under P1, with R read independently of the CMB and nothing tuned, how does the Molien shell spectrum of S³/2I score against ΛCDM on the Planck low-ℓ temperature likelihood, at each of MIT's two R routes? The pre-freeze expectation (§8) is that it scores far worse at route A and is unsettled at route B, so this is a quantitative refutation test of P1: it tests whether and by how much P1 fails at A, and whether it fails at B.

## 3. Qualification, gates, then the computation

**Qualification, done before the freeze.** The Commander file carries its own self-test: a test spectrum with a calibration value, and the log-likelihood recorded for it when the file was made. Loaded with the pinned clipy, the file returns ln L = −11.6257408575 against its recorded −11.62573983, a residual of −1.03 × 10⁻⁶ (`self_test.py`, `self_test.out`). The recorded value carries eight decimals, a rounding floor of 5 × 10⁻⁹, and the file's tables are stored in double precision, so the residual, about 200 times that floor, is a real difference between clipy and the code that wrote the file. It passes the standard stated here, 10⁻³ in ln L, which rests on one basis: a difference between implementations must be negligible next to the ±2 decision band. 10⁻³ is two thousand times smaller than the band, and the residual is about a thousand times smaller than 10⁻³. The standard was stated after the result was seen. The first run asserted 10⁻⁶ in ln L, a bound with no stated basis, and missed it by 3%; neither clipy nor Cobaya applies a tolerance of its own. This is code validation, not a gate: no spectrum of ours entered it, and every multipole of the test spectrum sits deep inside the table (largest Gaussianized |x| = 1.93), so it qualifies the interpolation only that far in.

Three gates then run in order after the freeze and before any P1 likelihood is evaluated, so that a failure has one location: the Planck interface, then CAMB, then the shell replacement. The first two use the baseline fit base_plikHM_TTTEEE_lowl_lowE, the one fit that carries a Commander term.

- **G1a, Planck interface.** That fit's packaged best-fit spectrum (its `.minimum.theory_cl`) and calibration, fed to the Commander likelihood through the pinned clipy, reproduce the Commander χ²_eff recorded in its `.minimum` file, 23.25721, to within 0.005: half a unit in the second decimal of the Planck results table, which prints this one as 23.26 (section 2.7). The Planck tables define χ²_eff = −2 ln L, and clipy returns ln L, so the comparison is −2 ln L against χ²_eff. The tolerance cannot be the file's own printed precision: the packaged spectrum carries six significant figures, which by itself limits the agreement to something of order 10⁻⁴. The basis for 0.005 is that it sits well above that rounding, and in ln L it is 0.0025, eight hundred times below the ±2 decision band, while the plumbing errors G1a exists to catch (a units slip, a misplaced multipole, a missing calibration) move χ²_eff by more.
- **G1b, CAMB.** CAMB 2.0.4, at that fit's parameters and with the frozen settings, reproduces its best-fit spectrum for 2 ≤ ℓ ≤ 29 to within 2 × 10⁻³ in each C_ℓ, and its Commander χ²_eff to within 0.2.
- **G2, shell replacement.** The shell-sum code at R = 200 χ*, where the gap sits far below ℓ = 2, reproduces the continuous CAMB spectrum for 2 ≤ ℓ ≤ 29 to within 10⁻³ in each C_ℓ, both for the full S³ spectrum (g_N = (N+1)², V = 2π²R³) and for the Molien spectrum; the same code with the 1/120 dropped must fail.

**A gate that fails.** A defect a gate finds may be corrected only inside the machinery that gate tests, with the diff and the reason appended as a dated erratum, and every gate then re-runs from G1a. No correction may change the frozen inputs, the prescription, the weighting, R, the statistic, the bands or the tolerances. A gate that still fails ends the run as Uninformative.

**Shell cutoff.** There is no fixed upper cutoff. It starts at N_max = 1024 and doubles until every C_ℓ, 2 ≤ ℓ ≤ 29, changes by less than 10⁻⁴ on two successive doublings, for A, for B and for the G2 fixture; if that has not happened by N_max = 2²¹, the gate fails. Every shell from N = 1 up to the cutoff is summed.

Then, at A and at B: the primary statistic, the secondary statistic, and one reported diagnostic, recorded in full. The diagnostic is the per-ℓ ratio C_ℓ(P1)/C_ℓ(ΛCDM) for 2 ≤ ℓ ≤ 29, together with, for every spectrum evaluated (ΛCDM, A and B) and every multipole, the Gaussianized value x_ℓ and where D_ℓ sits in the Commander table: interior (|x_ℓ| ≤ 4), edge (|x_ℓ| > 4 but inside) or outside. Because the self-test reached only |x| ≤ 1.93, an edge multipole is flagged in the report as unqualified interpolation, not presented as a clean number. The diagnostic is not scored and is no part of the Verdict. It is the measurement of how P1 fails, and the shape any later source-to-sky map would be measured against.

## 4. Hit, miss, inconclusive

- **Primary statistic.** Δ ln L = ln L(P1) − ln L(ΛCDM) on the Commander likelihood, every input identical except the shell selection. The Planck tables state that likelihood normalization is arbitrary; the difference between two theories under the same likelihood is what carries meaning.
- **Bands.** Positive: Δ ln L ≥ +2. Negative: Δ ln L ≤ −2. Inconclusive: anything between. The band is a preregistered decision convention (a likelihood ratio of about 7 either way), not a significance level; it is not "2σ". At A the statistic is expected far outside the band (§8), so there the Verdict is close to a formality and the diagnostic of §3 carries the information.
- **A and B.** A sets the Verdict. B gets its own label from the same band and is reported beside it. If they disagree, that is recorded as bearing on the route split in [The R Problem](r-problem.md), and neither replaces the other.
- **Out of support.** The Commander likelihood is tabulated over a finite range of D_ℓ at each multipole, recorded from the file in `commander_support.json`. When any D_ℓ lies outside it, clipy's `gibbs_lkl` returns the sentinel −1e30 (other implementations return −∞). The sentinel counts as Negative: for a P1 spectrum, Δ ln L is recorded as out of support rather than printed as a number, and the primary result is Negative, not an implementation failure. The report then gives the multipoles outside, their predicted D_ℓ, the bound each crosses, how many multipoles are outside and the largest fractional distance outside.
- **Secondary,** reported and never decisive: the ratio C_2/C_3, predicted against the published spectrum's value.
- **Fixed before the run, so not scored:** where the shells project. At χ* near 14 Gpc the first surviving shell (N = 12) sits near ℓ ≈ 29.5 at A and ℓ ≈ 9 at B, so at A the whole scored window lies below the first shell, though that shell's power reaches ℓ ≈ 25 to 29 (§8). [CMB Anomalies](../../../../cosmos/files/cmb-anomalies.md) §IV quotes the last empty shell (N = 10) instead, with 1/2 subtracted: near ℓ ≈ 24 at A and ℓ ≈ 7 at B.

## 5. Matched circles

Under P1 the circle observable is N/A, not a pass: with nothing identified there is nothing for a circle search to find, so an absence of circles is not evidence either way. The countermodel's circles are recorded in §0 with the work that excludes them: Planck 2013 XXVI, Key et al. 2007, and the first-year bound of Cornish et al. 2004 (no matched circles, back-to-back or nearly so, of radius above 25°).

## 6. Moves that do not count

Changing the prescription, the weighting, R, the ℓ range, the statistic, the codes or their settings, the parameters, a gate, a tolerance, the cutoff rule or the bands after any of the gates has run; choosing any of them for its expected fit; adding a statistic after the fact; counting the N/A circle observable as support; putting B in place of A; substituting another likelihood implementation after the freeze; reading R off the result, which would make it a CMB-derived R, the kind [The R Problem](r-problem.md) rules not independent. A different source-to-sky map enters with its derivation, before the freeze, or not at all.

## 7. After the run

The result is recorded whatever it is. The Test page carries the Verdict, set by A, and reports A and B side by side, each with its statistic or out-of-support record and its per-ℓ diagnostic; B's label is given in full beside A's, not as a footnote. If the frozen likelihood cannot be evaluated after the freeze for any reason other than a spectrum out of support, that is an infrastructure failure: the per-ℓ diagnostic is still reported, the primary statistic is recorded as unevaluated, the Verdict is Uninformative, and the reason is appended as a dated erratum; no other implementation is substituted. A runtime that differs from the frozen one ends the run the same way, before anything is evaluated. The falsification table in [CMB Anomalies](../../../../cosmos/files/cmb-anomalies.md) §VII gains one row, fixed now: the claim "under the P1 spectral-transfer prescription, the shell gap accounts for the low-ℓ deficit", falsified if Δ ln L ≤ −2 against ΛCDM at the coupling-route R, the out-of-support sentinel included. Its §VI gains one sentence: under the P1 spectral-transfer prescription the circle observable is N/A, with a link to the Test page. The general matched-circle position of that page is not changed by this test. [The Molien Shells](molien-shells.md) updates its status. [The R Problem](r-problem.md) may cite the result only as a consistency check on the route split, never as a route to R.

## 8. Expectation and caveats

**What is expected.** Two theory-only estimates were made before the freeze, both on scale-invariant power with χ* = 14 Gpc and no data; the full pipeline replaces them. The first, `sw_estimate.py`, keeps only the Sachs-Wolfe term. It puts route A's C_ℓ between 0.4% and 66% of ΛCDM's across 2 ≤ ℓ ≤ 20, with a median of 8%, and above ΛCDM's by up to a factor of three at ℓ ≈ 25 to 29, where the first shell's power lands. At route B it puts the quadrupole at 2% of ΛCDM's and ℓ = 3 to 5 between 15% and 27%, with an excess of up to 1.75 times at ℓ ≈ 7 to 8, between 0.5 and 1.3 times from ℓ = 9 to 20, and between 0.85 and 1.1 times above. The second, `isw_estimate.py`, a crude line of sight through a matter-plus-Λ background, adds the integrated Sachs-Wolfe term, the omission that matters most for route A. At the low multipoles the Sachs-Wolfe term samples the first shell's j_ℓ at kχ* ≈ 30, deep in its tail, while the integrated term samples j_ℓ(kr) at kr of a few, near its main peak, so it is far less suppressed. With it, route A's quadrupole rises to 11% of ΛCDM's, about ten times the Sachs-Wolfe figure, and its C_ℓ for 2 ≤ ℓ ≤ 10 to between 10% and 40%; route B's quadrupole rises to 36%, with ℓ = 7 to 8 still 1.5 to 1.7 times ΛCDM's. The integrated term is itself a minority of ΛCDM's own low-ℓ power in the same model, 38% of the quadrupole's auto-power and 11% by ℓ = 10, so it cannot lift route A to ΛCDM. The CMB page calls the observed low-ℓ features modest and "broadly consistent with ΛCDM". So route A is expected to be strongly disfavoured, and route B's outcome is not settled by these estimates. The test is run to measure that, not in hope of a fit; stating it before the freeze means nobody can later say the expectation was known and left out.

**A comparison made before the freeze.** One reviewer set the Sachs-Wolfe estimates above against the Commander support table and reported route A outside it at 15 of the 28 multipoles (ℓ = 2 to 13, 15, 16 and 19) and route B at two (ℓ = 2 and 5). It is recorded because it happened. It was not recomputed for this contract, and the table is derived from the data. The out-of-support clause, the bands and the choice of A to set the Verdict were fixed before it; the sole change made afterwards was to report B beside A rather than beneath it.

**What a result can show.** A negative result retires P1 at that route, not step one's Molien shells. A positive result would support the claim that the low-ℓ spectrum has power only at these wavenumbers, with these weights, and nothing stronger: under P1 the spatial pattern is discarded, so any model producing the same wavenumbers and multiplicities scores identically. A fit would not show that the source domain is S³/2I, or that MIT requires the spectral-only map.

**Caveats.** This is a preregistered retrospective test, not a blind prediction. MIT already knows the observed low-ℓ deficit and has discussed its approximate location; freezing the pipeline first prevents further tuning but cannot undo that prior contact. Below ℓ = 30 cosmic variance dominates, and each anomaly is 2 to 3σ and moves with the estimator and the mask ([CMB Anomalies](../../../../cosmos/files/cmb-anomalies.md) §I), which is why the statistic, the likelihood and the bands are fixed here first. Assumption 2 in §0 is part of the model: step two tests its consequences, not the assumption itself.

## 9. The freeze
