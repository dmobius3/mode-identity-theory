# ♲ CMB Anomalies

<img src="https://pbs.twimg.com/media/HLmA7TCXsAEUMR-?format=jpg&name=4096x4096" width="100%" alt="CMB Anamolies">

Four large-angle features in the CMB have persisted across COBE, WMAP, and Planck: a low-ℓ power deficit, an anomalously low quadrupole, an odd-over-even parity preference ($`R_{TT} \approx 0.81`$), and a quadrupole-octupole alignment (the "axis of evil"). Each is modest (2-3σ), sensitive to the estimator and the mask, broadly consistent with ΛCDM, and still awaiting a polarization counterpart; the interest is less any single feature than the sector that carries the tension. Cosmic topology has long been proposed as a common origin.

This page fixes what the Poincaré homology sphere $`S^3/2I`$ contributes. Two facts about the manifold hold regardless of cosmology: it is chiral (§III), carrying no orientation-reversing self-isometry, and its $`2I`$-invariant scalar spectrum has the Molien shell gap (§IV), empty across $`N = 2`$ to $`10`$ with its first invariant at $`N = 12`$. The line the page draws is this: **the topology gives the shell gap, and two things stand between that gap and the sky: the curvature radius $`R`$, which fixes its scale, and a spatial projection, which MIT does not yet supply.** Chirality shuts one route to parity, and the quotient's own symmetry shuts the alignment: its isotropy group $`A_5`$ forces the quadrupole isotropic and uncorrelated with the octupole, so a Gaussian source reaching the sky by an $`A_5`$-preserving map leaves their relative orientation uniform (§III). Parity alone stays open to the eigenmode covariance; $`R`$ is read from the coupling and mass-spectrum routes rather than the CMB (§V).

## I. The four anomalies

At the largest angles ($`\ell < 30`$), Planck analyses continue to report four features first seen by COBE and WMAP:

- **Low-ℓ deficit.** Less large-angle power than best-fit ΛCDM.
- **Low quadrupole.** $`C_2`$ low relative to $`C_3`$ and the higher multipoles.
- **Parity asymmetry.** Odd-ℓ power exceeds even-ℓ at large scales; the Planck TT ratio is $`R_{TT} \approx 0.81`$ (unity is no preference).
- **Quadrupole-octupole alignment.** The $`\ell = 2`$ and $`\ell = 3`$ preferred axes align to within $`{\sim}10°`$.

Each is 2-3σ on its own, moves with the choice of estimator and mask, sits inside the spread ΛCDM realizations produce, and awaits a confirming signal in polarization. The case rests on their persistence across missions and their shared home at the largest scales, not on any one being decisive.

## II. The manifold

The spatial manifold is $`M = S^3/2I`$, where $`2I`$ is the binary icosahedral group of order 120, the largest exceptional finite subgroup of $`\mathrm{SU}(2) \cong S^3`$. It inherits the round metric of constant positive curvature, has $`\pi_1(M) = 2I`$ and $`b_1 = 0`$, and is orientable as a three-manifold. As the Poincaré homology sphere $`\Sigma(2,3,5)`$ it is chiral (§III). The Möbius surface of the broader framework selects the twisted sector in which the first-positive seed $`\Lambda_\text{top}`$ is realized; it is separate from the CMB spectrum. The curvature radius $`R`$ enters the harmonic spectrum (§IV); its value is the open problem of §V.

## III. Chirality theorem

The structural result is that $`S^3/2I`$ is chiral.

Every orientation-reversing isometry of $`S^3`$ has the form $`q \mapsto u\,\bar{q}\,v`$ in quaternion coordinates (conjugation reverses order). Since $`2I`$ acts by left multiplication, any such map turns that left action into a right one, and for a non-central element a left translation stays distinct from a right one. So $`S^3/2I`$ admits no orientation-reversing self-isometry: it is chiral. This matches the known property of $`\Sigma(2,3,5)`$ and the general fact that every closed three-dimensional spherical space form is orientable.

What this closes is one route to parity. An even/odd-ℓ grading driven by an orientation-reversing identification, a Möbius boundary condition of the kind earlier versions of this page invoked, would need exactly the self-isometry the theorem rules out; that grading route is shut, and the $`2I`$-invariant spectrum inherits no intrinsic parity selector from it. Parity itself stays open: a compact quotient can still carry parity-odd and aligned structure through the anisotropic covariance of its eigenmodes, a route cosmic-topology work has established in general (the COMPACT collaboration among them), where the covariance produces parity-odd correlations without parity-violating microphysics. For $`S^3/2I`$ the two halves of that route have since parted. Parity is the open calculation, and stays open to this topology pending it, rather than being assigned to it or removed from it.

**The alignment corollary.** The quadrupole-octupole half is closed, by the quotient's symmetry rather than its chirality. $`S^3/2I`$ is homogeneous with isotropy group $`A_5`$ at every point, so a sky covariance commuting with $`A_5`$ is forced isotropic at $`\ell = 2`$, whose $`A_5`$ content is a single irreducible, and forced to vanish between $`\ell = 2`$ and $`\ell = 3`$, which share no constituent. An isotropic quadrupole independent of the octupole leaves their relative orientation uniform. So a Gaussian source carried to the sky by any map preserving the observer's $`A_5`$ produces no quadrupole-octupole alignment beyond what full isotropy gives: the feature is not this topology's to supply, and the covariance route reaches parity alone. Outside the corollary sit a non-Gaussian source and a map that breaks the observer's $`A_5`$, the second being where [the Oort Cloud project](../../framework/files/working/files/oort-cloud-project.md)'s local-sampling reading of the alignment now has to live. The derivation, with the per-$`\ell`$ table of which spins may be anisotropic and which pairs may couple, and its script check, is [The Molien Shells: The P1 Bridge](../../framework/files/working/files/molien-p1-bridge.md) §II.

## IV. The Molien shell gap

Scalar harmonics on $`S^3`$ are graded by polynomial degree $`N = 0, 1, 2, \ldots`$ with eigenvalue $`N(N+2)/R^2`$ and degeneracy $`(N+1)^2`$. On $`S^3/2I`$ only $`2I`$-invariant harmonics survive; the centre $`\{-1\}`$ acts on degree $`N`$ by $`(-1)^N`$, restricting invariants to even $`N`$. Among these the Molien series of the invariant ring is

```math
P(t) = \frac{1 - t^{60}}{(1 - t^{12})(1 - t^{20})(1 - t^{30})},
```

with generators at degrees 12, 20, and 30 (the three Klein icosahedral invariants). The first nontrivial invariant is at $`N = 12`$; the shells $`N = 2, 4, 6, 8, 10`$ are empty, and the surviving shells run $`N = 0, 12, 20, 24, 30, \ldots`$. This shell structure is topological and fixed, a spectral fact about $`S^3/2I`$ that holds independent of cosmology. What each surviving shell carries is worked out in [The Molien Shells](../../framework/files/working/files/molien-shells.md): up to $`N = 30`$ each holds a single invariant form, whose roots on the sphere are the icosahedron's vertices ($`N = 12`$), face centres ($`N = 20`$) and edge midpoints ($`N = 30`$), with $`N = 24`$ the square of the first.

Where the gap lands on the sky depends on $`R`$. Each level $`N`$ sits at wavenumber $`k_N = \sqrt{N(N+2)}/R`$, which the projection estimate carries to a characteristic multipole, $`\ell_\mathrm{char} + \tfrac{1}{2} \simeq k_N \chi_*`$, with $`\chi_*`$ the comoving distance to last scattering. That relation is an estimate rather than an identity; the exact multipole mapping comes from the transfer functions. The boundary set by the last empty shell ($`N = 10`$) scales as $`1/R`$: it falls near $`\ell \approx 28`$ at the measured-$`\Lambda`$ back-read $`R \approx 5.38`$ Gpc and near $`\ell \approx 24`$ at the coupling route's $`R \approx 6.1`$ Gpc. The reach of the deficit into the observed range ($`\ell \lesssim 30`$) therefore rides on $`R`$. The coupling route is the best-conditioned *independent* reading and it lands the boundary in range, the $`5.38`$ Gpc figure being the circular back-read of measured $`\Lambda`$ rather than a route. The mass spectrum reads $`R`$ independently too, but far more coarsely: its stated uncertainty spans most of the low-$`\ell`$ window, so it does not supply a competing cutoff location ([the R problem](../../framework/files/working/files/r-problem.md)). The low quadrupole sits inside this same thinning; its $`C_2/C_3`$ value waits on the full projection (transfer functions, primordial weighting, observer position), held apart from the shell-gap result. Under MIT's frozen shell-to-sky rule, where a shell's spectrum crosses to the sky but its directional pattern does not (P1 in the working notes), the low-ℓ spectrum at the coupling and electron-muon radii, computed with the full transfer function, is tabulated in [The Molien Shells: The Full-Transfer Ratio Table](../../framework/files/working/files/molien-ratio-table.md), as a theory result not scored against data.

That arithmetic carries a level's wavenumber to a multipole, which presumes a map taking the quotient's fields to fields on flat effective space. The topology fixes the gap; between the gap and the sky stand the curvature scale $`R`$, which fixes the wavenumbers, and that projection, which the framework does not yet derive. The frozen rule fixes how much power each shell puts on the sky, not how the quotient's fields become fields on flat space, and the numbers it yields, here and in the ratio table, stand as computed. What is missing is a reason for the rule. A construction from the machinery MIT already has produced no spatial map at all ([The Missing Spatial Projection](../../framework/files/working/files/missing-spatial-projection.md)), and no map from the quotient's own fields to fields on flat space produces the rule either, in the class tested: a deterministic linear one taken shell by shell, with each shell required to contribute the same everywhere. The shells that do admit an icosahedral alternative, at $`N = 20`$ and $`N = 30`$, cannot cover $`N = 12`$, so no replacement assembles ([The Spatial Carriers](../../framework/files/working/files/spatial-carriers.md)). What has closed is the route through the existing geometry, not the possibility of a projection, and the state of the question is sharper than unfinished work: MIT constrains such an object, by the symmetry it must inherit, by locality, by the scales already fixed and by the phase ratio's time dependence, and supplies nothing that selects one ([The Effective-Metric Floors](../../framework/files/working/files/effective-metric-floors.md)). None of this vacates §III. The alignment corollary asks only that the map preserve the observer's $`A_5`$, so it binds whatever object eventually supplies the projection, provided it does, and it binds the frozen prescription too, which preserves the full rotation group and $`A_5`$ with it.

## V. The curvature scale

The shell structure of §IV is fixed; what $`R`$ sets is where it lands, and $`R`$ is the framework's open problem rather than something the CMB supplies.

The operative cosmology is flat FLRW and CMB-consistent: spatial curvature enters as a static boundary condition rather than a Friedmann term, so the apparent $`\Omega_K`$ is zero and light propagates on flat-equivalent distances $`D_M = \chi_*`$. An earlier version of this analysis read $`R`$ off the deficit boundary, took it as a physical spatial-curvature radius, and applied the curved projection $`D_M = R\sin(\chi/R)`$, which moved the first acoustic peak to $`\ell \approx 42`$ and implied $`\Omega_\mathrm{tot} \approx 1.70`$; that step was a coordinate error, since the flat-FLRW distance relation leaves the acoustic scale and $`\Omega_\mathrm{tot}`$ at their measured values.

So the Molien gap fixes the shell structure, and $`R`$ fixes where that structure lands. Two independent routes read $`R`$, and they read it differently. The coupling ($`\alpha`$) route fixes $`\Omega_\Lambda`$, hence $`R \approx 6.1`$ Gpc and $`\Lambda_\text{ref}`$ about 23% below the measured value, which lands the gap near $`\ell \approx 24`$, inside the observed band. The mass spectrum reads $`R`$ independently but too coarsely to place the boundary: its span covers most of the low-$`\ell`$ window, so it neither confirms nor contradicts that landing. How the two readings relate, and whether their displacement is conditioning or a real disagreement, is tracked in [the R problem](../../framework/files/working/files/r-problem.md). The radial hinge is falsifiable within any fixed projection prescription: the deficit scale must follow the independently read $`R`$, and the two figures above are what the frozen rule makes of those radii. The second hinge is whether MIT derives the projection that carries those scales to the sky.

## VI. Matched circles

A compact quotient can produce matched circles where the last-scattering sphere meets its topological copies. Planck's searches returned a null over the topology classes and circle statistics tested, which constrains standard compact FLRW geometries whose fundamental domain reaches the last-scattering surface. This framework uses the topology as a global boundary condition while keeping CMB propagation flat-FLRW, so the standard curved-space circle exclusion describes a different geometry than the one here. The model therefore owes its own circle prediction, computed from the same $`R`$, transfer functions, and boundary prescription that set the low-ℓ spectrum. Until that calculation runs, the matched-circle null stays a consistency check, sitting to one side of both support and exclusion. Under the frozen shell-to-sky rule, which identifies no points, the circle observable is N/A ([The Molien Shells, Step Two](../../framework/files/working/files/molien-step-two.md)).

## VII. Falsification

| Claim | Falsified if |
|---|---|
| $`S^3/2I`$ is chiral | An orientation-reversing self-isometry of $`S^3/2I`$ is exhibited |
| The orientation-reversing grading route to parity is shut | A parity grading is derived from that route on the orientable quotient |
| The quadrupole-octupole alignment is not this topology's, for a Gaussian source under an $`A_5`$-preserving map | Such a construction on $`S^3/2I`$ is exhibited that correlates the $`\ell = 2`$ and $`\ell = 3`$ axes, or makes the quadrupole anisotropic |
| Molien shell gap: first invariant at $`N = 12`$, shells 2-10 empty | A $`2I`$-invariant harmonic appears at $`N = 2, 4, 6, 8`$, or $`10`$ |
| Under the frozen shell-to-sky rule, the deficit lands at the observed multipoles | The independently read $`R`$, carried through the frozen rule, places the cutoff outside the observed $`\ell \lesssim 30`$ band |
| A deterministic linear field map, taken shell by shell with each shell contributing the same everywhere, can place a shell's power on only finitely many directions | Such a map is exhibited that spreads a shell's power continuously over directions instead |
| Under the frozen shell-to-sky rule, the shell gap accounts for the low-ℓ deficit | The registered likelihood test crosses its pre-set rejection threshold in favour of ΛCDM at the coupling-route $`R`$ |

That likelihood test has run. It stopped before this model was ever scored, so it returned no verdict, and the criterion stands unscored ([The Molien Shells, Step Two](../../framework/files/working/files/molien-step-two.md)).

---

The accounting is the result. Two facts about $`S^3/2I`$ hold independent of cosmology: it is chiral, and its $`2I`$-invariant scalar spectrum carries the Molien gap across $`N = 2`$ to $`10`$. From there the four anomalies sort. MIT reads the low-ℓ deficit, with the low quadrupole inside it, as the observational counterpart of the shell gap. Where that gap sits in wavenumber rides on the independently read $`R`$; which multipoles it reaches takes a projection as well, and under the frozen rule the coupling route, the better-conditioned one, places the last empty level near $`\ell \approx 24`$ and lands the deficit in band, while the mass spectrum checks the same scaling structure independently but is too weakly conditioned to locate the cutoff. The parity asymmetry stays open to this topology through the eigenmode covariance, a calculation still to run. The quadrupole-octupole alignment no longer does: the quotient's $`A_5`$ isotropy forces the quadrupole isotropic and uncorrelated with the octupole, leaving the two axes' relative orientation uniform for any Gaussian source that reaches the sky by an $`A_5`$-preserving map, so that feature is not this geometry's to explain. The matched-circle null stays a consistency check, pending the framework's own circle prediction. And two hinges stay open: the curvature radius, read from the coupling and mass-spectrum routes rather than the CMB, and the projection that would carry the shell spectrum to flat effective space, which the search through the existing geometry has closed without supplying.

*The shell gap is the topology's to give. $`R`$ says where its scale belongs; the missing projection says whether and how that structure reaches the sky.*
